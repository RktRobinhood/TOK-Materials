What gives something its status as art?
Your jobChoose one position, then write 2–3 sentences that make a claim and use one specific example. Minimum 30 words.
Why start here?This is a baseline opinion, not a TOK assessment question. You will compare it with your final judgement to see what your reasoning changed.
Writing basic: your claim is usually your topic sentence. It should answer the question straight away — not announce what you are going to discuss.

02 / KNOWLEDGE FRAMEWORKScope

Can art actually know?

When does an artwork become a source of knowledge rather than merely an object to experience?
Do thisAnswer the knowledge question using the film or your other chosen arts example. Build one short paragraph using the five writing moves below, then choose one TOK concept that best names the knowledge issue. Minimum 48 words.
Assessment transferEssay move: make the example do analytical work for the exact question instead of retelling it. Exhibition move: keep the object/case specific and state its real-world context.
Arts recapArt can disclose experience, perspective, pattern and possibility without behaving like a scientific report.
The pressure pointMeaning is not automatically knowledge. You still need a reason to trust what you think the work shows.
Boundary testAsk what the artwork lets you know that a plain description might not — and what it cannot establish.
Use these five moves — topic sentence → evidence → explain → counterpoint → judgement + so what?
1 · Topic sentence / claimAnswer the knowledge question in one clear sentence.An artwork can become knowledge when…
2 · EvidencePoint to one specific moment, detail or example.In my case, the detail that matters is…
3 · ExplainSay exactly how that evidence supports your topic sentence.This supports my claim because…
4 · CounterpointGive a reasonable problem, limit or different view.However, this may only show… because…
5 · Judgement + so what?Decide how far your claim survives, then say what follows if you are right.So I would argue… This matters because…
Unrelated worked example — school phone rulePhones should be allowed during lunch because students need some control over how they use their break. For example, a student may need to message a parent about transport home. This supports the claim because lunch is not teaching time, so the reason for a classroom ban is weaker. However, unrestricted phone use could reduce face-to-face interaction. Therefore a lunch-only rule is stronger than a total ban; this means school rules should distinguish between learning time and break time.

03 / KNOWLEDGE FRAMEWORKPerspectives

Who gets the last word?

When interpretations of art conflict, what can make one interpretation better supported than another?
Do thisCompare at least two reasonable interpretations. Decide which is better supported by reasons and evidence, then explain what that means for how we judge interpretations. Then choose one TOK concept. Minimum 50 words.
Assessment transferEssay move: acknowledge and evaluate a genuinely different point of view. Exhibition move: explain how this particular object helps us think about the prompt; “it is interesting” is not enough.
Arts recapCreator, audience, critic, institution and culture may all see different things in the same work.
The pressure point“Everyone has an opinion” does not mean every interpretation is equally well supported.
JustificationLook for attention to the work, relevant context, coherent reasoning and evidence — not merely confidence or popularity.
Use these five moves — topic sentence → evidence → explain → counterpoint → judgement + so what?
1 · Topic sentence / claimGive your answer to the question immediately.A stronger interpretation is one that…
2 · EvidenceName two specific readings or pieces of support.One reading is… Another is…
3 · ExplainShow why your evidence makes one reading better supported.The first/second is stronger because…
4 · CounterpointShow why the other reading is not simply stupid or impossible.However, the other reading still has force because…
5 · Judgement + so what?State your standard and what it means for interpretation.I would rank them by… This means…

04 / KNOWLEDGE FRAMEWORKMethods & tools

The tool is never innocent

How far do the tools used to create, edit, copy and distribute art shape the knowledge we take from it?
Do thisPick one method or tool and trace how it changes what an audience can notice, know or trust. Decide what follows if the tool is changed or removed. Then choose one TOK concept. Minimum 50 words.
Assessment transferEssay move: analyse how knowledge is produced in the Arts, not merely that technology exists. Exhibition move: explain why this particular object/medium contributes something distinctive to the prompt.
Arts recapMedium and technique do more than package an idea: they can change what can be noticed, repeated, manipulated or shared.
The pressure pointA camera, edit, algorithm, gallery wall or printing process can reveal something while also filtering it.
Tool testImagine the same idea made with a different tool. If the knowledge changes, the method is part of the claim.
Use these five moves — topic sentence → evidence → explain → counterpoint → judgement + so what?
1 · Topic sentence / claimAnswer how far the tool matters.The tool shapes knowledge by…
2 · EvidenceName one specific tool, edit, process or distribution choice.The relevant tool/process in my case is…
3 · ExplainShow what the tool changes for the audience.This changes what the audience can know/notice because…
4 · CounterpointTest whether the idea would survive a different medium or tool.However, if the same idea used… then…
5 · Judgement + so what?Decide whether the method is part of the knowledge and say what follows.So the tool is / is not part of what is known because… Therefore…

05 / KNOWLEDGE FRAMEWORKEthics

Who pays for the picture?

How should ethical responsibilities affect the ways art represents and produces knowledge about real people, events or communities?
Do thisShow how an ethical choice changes what an audience can reasonably know, trust or understand. Then test your answer against artistic freedom and explain what this means for the audience. Choose one TOK concept. Minimum 50 words.
Assessment transferEssay move: keep it about knowledge — explain how ethics changes what can be known, trusted or represented, not only whether something is good or bad. Exhibition move: anchor the ethical issue in the object’s specific real-world context and explain its contribution to the prompt.
Arts recapArt can expose, distort, honour, exploit, provoke or humanise. Because representation can become knowledge for an audience, ethical choices affect what is believed as well as what is shown.
The pressure pointOffence, harm, censorship and responsibility are not the same thing. Keep them separate.
Responsibility testAsk who has the power to frame the subject, what knowledge is created or distorted, and what obligations follow from that power.
Use these five moves — topic sentence → evidence → explain → counterpoint → judgement + so what?
1 · Topic sentence / claimAnswer how ethics changes knowledge or trust.Ethical responsibility matters to knowledge when…
2 · EvidenceName one specific framing, representation or production choice.In my case, the choice that matters is…
3 · ExplainShow how that choice changes what an audience can know, trust or understand.This affects knowledge because…
4 · CounterpointGive artistic freedom its strongest reasonable case.However, artistic freedom still matters because…
5 · Judgement + so what?Set a limit and say why it matters for audiences.So I would trust/reject/qualify the work when… This matters because…

Exit through the argument

You have used the four knowledge-framework lenses. Now compress them into one judgement.

work / form / techniquecontext / reputation / institutions / market
To what extent is what we know through art shaped by the stories, reputations and institutions surrounding the work?
Do thisMake one clear judgement with a limit or condition. Use at least one specific piece of evidence and one of the four framework lenses, address one reasonable different view, then state one “so what?” consequence (an implication) of your conclusion. Minimum 70 words.
Essay transferSustain focus on the exact question; use examples as evidence; evaluate another point of view; explain a “so what?” implication; and finish with a clear judgement that includes a limit or condition. Those are the habits this card is deliberately rehearsing.
Use these five moves — topic sentence → evidence → explain → other reasonable view → judgement + so what?
1 · Topic sentence / claimAnswer “to what extent” immediately. This is your paragraph’s main point.To a large/some/limited extent, what we know through art is shaped by…
2 · EvidenceUse one specific example, not a general reference to the film.The strongest specific example is…
3 · ExplainUse one of the four framework lenses to explain why the example supports your claim.This supports my answer because the lens of… shows…
4 · Other reasonable viewGive a reasonable different answer and why someone could hold it.However, someone could argue… because…
5 · Judgement + so what?Return to your answer, add a limit, then state what follows if you are right.Overall… although… If this is right, then…

Translate this into assessment

TOK essay: you would answer one prescribed title through areas of knowledge. Keep the line of argument focused, use specific examples analytically, evaluate another point of view, consider implications, and show where the conclusion has limits.

TOK exhibition: one specific artwork/object from today could become one object; a broader case or controversy would need to be represented by a specific object. A full exhibition needs three specific objects, each in its specific real-world context, all linked explicitly to one selected IA prompt. For each object, you must justify what that particular object contributes to exploring the prompt. You do not simply write a general answer to the prompt.

When destruction adds value
Art market evidenceSotheby's

Girl with Balloon partially shredded itself immediately after selling at auction in 2018 and became Love is in the Bin. Sotheby's later sold the transformed work for £18.582 million in 2021.

Use it for: Where does artistic value come from? The object itself, the artist's action, the story, institutional recognition, scarcity, publicity — or some combination?
