THE MODEL EXPLAINS
Equal start. Fair odds.

The rules give both sides a 50/50 chance in each trade. That does not promise equal final wealth.

Try 20 flips, then 400. Watch the richest four's share.
Pause and explain: what did the coin promise?

After some flips, choose the most accurate claim.

Open the rulebook and check the mathematics

One trade: randomly choose two different people. Calculate 25% (or your chosen stake) of the poorer person's coins. Flip a fair coin. Transfer that many coins from loser to winner. Everyone starts equal, no coins are created, and no one has a higher chance of winning the flip.

Why imbalance can grow: the stake is limited by the poorer person's funds. A person with very few coins cannot quickly recover a large fortune. The result is a feature of this particular exchange model, not proof that every economic system must behave this way. This is a simplified yard-sale exchange model, also discussed by SIAM.

Two different puzzles with a fair coin

In the town game, coins move between people. In the next puzzle, the prize doubles when heads takes longer to appear. Both challenge intuition, but for different mathematical reasons.

What do these measurements mean?

Top and bottom shares: rank households by wealth, then add the richest or poorest four out of twenty. Gini: inequality measure from zero (everyone equal) toward one (nearly one person owns everything). Under 30: counts how many households cross an arbitrary model threshold, not a real poverty line. Top income: the biggest share of the last season's wage pool. Wealth and income are not interchangeable.

Each season in this toy world:
  1. Ten pairs exchange resources; a fair coin decides who wins an amount linked to the poorer partner's resources.
  2. All households contribute 5% of their holdings to a fixed wage pool. Pay depends on skill, access, luck, and any cumulative advantage.
  3. A wage cap, if selected, limits pay per household and returns the excess equally.
  4. The wealth tax and minimum floor redistribute existing resources. No outside money, debt, inflation or growth is modelled.

Every season preserves exactly 2,000 points. These are designed rules, not evidence that actual tax policies have the same effects in real economies. A model is useful when its assumptions and limits are visible.
