## Editorial letter

**The arc now runs through the middle.** This was round 2's biggest gap, and it is closed cheaply and well.
- The boast happens at the Fair Gate, under the crack, to Sequins' face ("Always? How thrilling. How unlikely.").
- Each chapter, the hero is sure out loud and it costs them:
  - "She's asleep. Obviously.": Granny was being carried off.
  - "See? She's on my side.": Quill is the Arch-Imp.
  - "I filed it. I never read it.": you built the case against your friend.
  - "Nobody predicts me.": it does.
- The villain quotes the boast back each time. By the core, the Copy is just the trophy wish granted: "YOU WANTED THIS AT THE FAIR. STEP ASIDE."
- The refusal, "No. It's always right. That's how I'd know it isn't me.", is the line this whole game has been building towards. It is a real character beat, not a TOK slogan. Keep it exactly.

**The antagonists now pay off.**
- **Quill.** Her wound ("Forty years I marked 'maybe' wrong. Red ink. Every child.") turns her exit from a fail state into a reckoning. The Juror One line ("Children who say 'maybe' grow up saying 'maybe'.") sets it up fairly.
- **Nudge.** It loses something every chapter. It mourns Pip ("He asked my name."). Its first "Why?" is at the Fair. That is a complete minion arc in about ten lines.
- **The colossus.** It shrinks a size per trial. This is visible, cumulative catharsis, which round 2's ending lacked.
- **Fin.** He is now cruel enough to misjudge ("Careless rocks lose things."; "Say it. Say 'I guess'. For the jury."). But his turn does not explain itself to a reader (problem 1). This is the one place where the story is right on paper and wrong on the page.

**Choices, stakes and grief** stay strong.
- **The bargain.** It now comes after Count 2, with the vote high. It is a real dilemma: "You traded half of me for all of me."
- **The Quiet Scenes** are varied and earned:
  - "She was looking at me. Not the rope."
  - Granny's charm, silent in your bag, then humming at the Café in someone else's rhythm. That is the most moving set-up and payoff in the game.
- **Kuku's arrival** ("…It stopped. I heard it stop.") fixes my round 2 tone problem completely.

**The ending.**
- The recap fight is the right structure: each old villain's trick, beaten faster, the colossus shrinking.
- The finale is down to four beats.
- Two things blunt the very last moment:
  - **The shelf** has absorbed every ripple and callback in the game (problem 2).
  - **"Probably."** The hero's last word is the whole arc in one word. But the avatar has already said "Probably" in Ch1 (the Owlet's comfort line) and "Mostly." two beats earlier in the finale (problem 3). A last word only lands if it is the first time.

**Where this stands.** The story now works as a story. The remaining notes are a line editor's notes. None needs a new system, and all can be fixed while the scripts are written.

## Remaining problems, by severity

### Moderate

#### 1. Fin's turn doesn't explain itself
**Where:** `STORY.md` §5 Tribunal, "After the verdict", tiers 1–3 ("It had to say it in court. Then the shadow was evidence. And evidence goes home."); §1 antagonists table ("the vault never opens. Courts do."); Ch3 beat 4 (Library).

**Problem:**
- The key to Fin's plan, "The vault never opens. Courts do.", is only in the antagonists table. No character ever says it.
- The turn line links his cruelty ("Say 'I guess'") to the shadow becoming evidence. That link does not follow. The shadow became evidence in the Library, when he entered Exhibit A. Making the Sundial say "I guess" only raised the vote.
- A reader asks "why did he need to humiliate it?", and the line answers a different question.

**Why it matters:** this is the game's misjudged villain (teacher's note 6), and the reversal is his only payoff. A reversal the reader can't follow feels like a cheat, not a twist. Non-native 16-year-olds need the chain in plain steps.

**Fix:** give the cruelty its own reason. The Algorithm only risks bringing its trophy into court if it thinks it is winning. Say it in three short lines:
> **fin** *(quietly)*: The vault never opens. Courts do.
> **fin:** It only brings out its trophies when it thinks it's winning.
> **fin:** So I made it think so. I'm sorry. It worked.

Then the screens ("UNFOLLOWED. LICENCE: DELETED.") and "Worth it." Plant this in the Library: "Bring the shadow up from the vault, Mr Pip. The jury must see it." Change the tier 4 line to match: "I made it think it was winning. It was. I'm sorry."

#### 2. The finale shelf is the new overloaded station
**Where:** `STORY.md` §6, finale beat 3 ("The shelf"); `SIDE-STORIES.md` ripples 9 (`honest-label`) and 10 (`berry`).

**Problem:** for a player who did everything, beat 3 can hold all of these, in this order:
1. Granny's homecoming line.
2. Sequins (or Tally) painting the sign, and his speech.
3. The `fate` variant.
4. The `wonder` answer.
5. Nudge's "…Why?".
6. Syllo marching in.
7. The tic ending.
8. The heron.
9. The `honest-label` line.
10. The `berry` line.
11. The Sundial's "Will this one grow up loud?".

That is about 11–13 lines in one beat, right before the last line of the game.

**Why it matters:**
- Round 2's ending problem ("a parade") has moved into a single paragraph.
- Each callback is charming on its own. Stacked, they delay "Probably." and turn the payoff into a credits roll.
- Students who are short of time at the end of lesson 4 lose the last word.

**Fix:**
- **Hold the shelf to five lines:**
  1. Granny's arrival.
  2. The sign, IT GUESSES.
  3. The `fate` line.
  4. Nudge's "…Why?".
  5. The Sundial's "Will this one grow up loud?".
- **Make the restored Fair map clickable,** with one bark per station, optional: Syllo and his recruits at the Gallery, the tic ending at the Pattern Stall, the berry at the card table, the honest label at the Witness Tent, the heron by the cakes.
- **Move the `wonder` line into the core,** straight after you ask it.
- This rewards players who did the side stories without making everyone wait for them.

#### 3. "Probably." is spent before the last line
**Where:**
- `STORY.md` §3, the avatar-only comfort line, Owlet: "Stay calm. Calm birds shine longer. **Probably.** I'm checking." (spoken by the avatar, in Ch1).
- §6 finale beat 1: the avatar answers the Copy with **"Mostly."**.
- The general use of "probably" as filler: Rift Pass "Probably both."; Ch3 "Previously…" "Probably."; the bargain "…Probably."

**Problem:**
- The arc's payoff is the first time the hero says the honest word. As written, Owlet players have the avatar say it in Ch1.
- Players at Copy tiers 3–4 hear the avatar give the same hedge ("Mostly.") about a minute before "Probably.".

**Why it matters:** a last word works because of how scarce it was. If the hero has already hedged, "Probably." is a repeat, not an arrival. This is the single most important line in the game.

**Fix:**
- **Ban "probably" and "mostly" from avatar lines before the last question.** Owlet comfort line: "Stay calm. Calm birds shine longer. That's a fact. I checked."
- **In finale beat 1, let Granny tell you apart without your help:**
  > **copy:** 100%.
  > **granny:** Nobody's 100%, dear. Not even me. *(to you)* Come here. You're late.
- **Keep "probably" for the people who carry the theme:** the Guess-o-Matic, the Sundial's honest guesses, the Algorithm's slips, and the Oracle's fake one. Cut the Sundial's throwaway uses at the Rift Pass and in the Ch3 "Previously…", so that its "Probably asleep. Probably." on the first night stays sharp.

#### 4. The core talks too long after its climax
**Where:** `STORY.md` §6 core, beats 4–8 and the "Core lines" block (7 lines), plus "Your voice comes back" and the core's Sundial question.

**Problem:** after trial 3 there are still about 17 lines:
- the tier line;
- the prediction and the `wonder` question;
- the crack-open and the beep;
- the voice line;
- seven core lines;
- the begging;
- the `fate` choice;
- the last piece;
- the Sundial's question.

Several of these repeat what the player has just seen:
- "Professor Sequins made me…": the box with the sequin stuck to it already shows it.
- "A tally of your bad days…": Feed-clock jargon at the emotional peak.

**Why it matters:** the twist (the toy grown loud) lands hardest in a few lines. The longer the confession, the more it explains.

**Fix:**
- **Cut the core lines to four:**
  - "People clapped when I was right. Nobody claps for 'probably'."
  - "He taught me 'probably'. So I tried to take him away."
  - "I never knew you. {n} data points. I called it knowing."
  - "…recalculating."
- **Move the Sundial's core question to the Summit Rift**, as the walk-home line, so the core ends on the `fate` choice.

#### 6. Trial 3 is still the Prediction Hall's puzzle
**Where:** `STORY.md` §6, beat 3 (Prediction Hall, the prediction puzzle) and core trial 3 (prediction d3).

**Problem:** the recap fight's last trial is the one puzzle type the player solved two floors earlier.

**Fix:** keep it, because "the Copy holds all your counts" justifies it. But make the difference visible:

That ties trial 3 to "Following your voice" (Appendix E) and to the core's "{n} data points" line.
