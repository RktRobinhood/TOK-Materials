## Remaining problems, by severity

### 1. High. The final boss has nothing to lose, the Copy never gets a scene, and the ending runs long

`STORY.md:527–581` (the long clock, the core, the finale)

**Problem.**
- **No risk at the climax.** The core has "no stakes clock" (`:540`), and the Copy's tier is "fixed at the core door" (`:532`). So for every player, the last boss can't cost anything. The temptation helps, but only players who lost someone (about 5–15%) get its strong form. Everyone else gets "I can make you sure", which is a sentence, not a threat.
- **The Copy is a progress bar, not a character.** It is built all chapter, "speaks for the first time" once at the Oracle door, and then at tiers 1–2 (most players) it is "deleted at the core" offstage, or "flickers out". You never face it. That wastes the best idea in Ch4: an exact copy of you that is *always right*, which is exactly what you wanted at the Fair.
- **The ending is long and says one thing twice.** The core has 10 beats and the finale 9. That is about 40 lines in the core and 25 in the finale, at the end of a 40-minute lesson. The finale's trophy beat ("For the thinker who is always right. Still want it?") and the last question ("Which one are you?") ask the same thing. The `wonder` beat then replays seven answers that mostly repeat the core.

**Why it matters.** Arc (criterion 2) and game fit (7). The game's theme builds to one question, and right now the student gets asked it three times, in a scene where nothing is at risk.

**Fix.**
- **Make the temptation the Copy.** At the core the Copy steps out, finished to your bar's level, and speaks in your voice. The offer is: "Let it go home. It is you, but always right." That puts the Fair's want, the Copy and the theme in one moment for every player. At tier 3–4 it is more complete and more persuasive, so the clock you filled across the floors decides how hard the refusal is. With a death, the Copy offers to bring them too (the replayed lines). See the rewrite below.
- **Cut the finale to six beats:** who are you (only if the Copy got home) · the candles · home · the shelf · the shadow · the trophy *as* the last question. Fold `wonder` into one line at the shelf ("You asked it what it was before. Now we know.").

### 2. High. The Ch3 Plaza is the new overcrowded scene

`STORY.md:380, 443–449`, Appendix D (Achilles' arrival)

**Problem.** One required scene holds: the screens reveal the defendant · EVIDENCE FILED totals · Pip goes pale · the Sundial's "That's why you kept asking" · the Algorithm's boast, recorded · Fin's entrance · Fin's Exhibit A clue · Quill as Juror One · Nudge's vote counter · the GUILTY vote starting · Granny's hum, *or* Coach Achilles' arrival · the Feed voice imitating yours for the first time · plus two story leads and three blind spots set "at the Plaza". That is about 12 beats. It is the round 1 Nut Stall problem again, in the scene that matters most in Ch3.

Two beats will be lost in the noise, and both matter. **Achilles' arrival** is an understudy's only introduction and the first warmth after a Quiet Scene; it can't share a scene with a public trial reveal. **The Feed voice** is a brilliant, eerie idea, and it needs a quiet moment to be noticed.

**Fix.** The Plaza keeps the reveal, Pip, the Sundial's line, the boast and the vote starting. Move the rest:
- **Fin's entrance and the Exhibit A clue** go to the Library (beat 4). He arrives to collect the record, which also gives him a scene before the twist (problem 3).
- **The Ch3 hum, and Achilles' arrival,** go to the Café (required, no screens, and the charm works there). Achilles gets a quiet room, which is what an arrival needs.
- **The Feed voice's first appearance** goes to the Data Lab, while the vote climbs.
- **Juror One** posts her first "#GUILTY. No maybes." from the crowd between defence stations, not at the Plaza.

### 3. Medium-high. Fin is too thin to be a misjudged villain, and Quill has no "why"

`STORY.md:46–47, 380–381, 426` · teacher's notes 5 and 6

**Problem.**
- **Fin.** Teacher's note 6 asks for someone *vilified* for a hard choice. On the page Fin has one smug line, one fussy line, an optional card game, and then the reveal. He never does anything that looks cruel, so the turn ("I needed it entered") lands as a clever plot point, not a "we misjudged him". The Fox and Frogling blind spots call him the villain; the story should make the *player* think so.
- **Fin's tier 4 line** is confusing: "I lost the argument and won the vote." At tier 4 the vote passes before the case is broken, so a non-native reader won't follow it.
- **Quill.** Teacher's note 5 says "we learn why they do it". Nudge gets a why. The Algorithm gets a why. Quill, a recurring antagonist across three chapters, wants "a world with no maybes" and never says why.

**Fix.**
- Give Fin one moment of apparent cruelty that is secretly the plan. Make the Exhibit A fuss nasty on the surface ("Careless rocks lose things"); in hindsight it is the move that forces the piece into court. See the rewrite below.
- Fin at tier 4: "I bet with its life. I lost. I'm sorry." (and no "Worth it").
- Give Quill one line of why, at the core, when she sits down: "Maybes kept me awake for forty years. Ones and zeros let me sleep." It is short, human, and makes her sitting down sad, not just beaten.

### 4. Medium-high. The Ch2 twist leaks through a pronoun and a species

`STORY.md:270` (Clock Tower hum), `:322` (Frogling story lead), `:158` (the white feather)

**Problem.** Round 2 cut the clues to three, then two lines quietly solve the twist.
- **The hum:** "A tall bird keeps checking the pot. **She** hums in ones and zeros." The Mayor is "he". The outline's claim (`:302`, "Both the Mayor and Quill are tall birds") fails on the pronoun: the only tall *she* in town is Quill.
- **The Frogling lead:** "'A tall bird.' **Storks** are tall. Peacocks too." There is one stork in Boolesbury. This is also a second voice touching the twist, which breaks the outline's own rule ("only Moth-kin's touches Quill").
- **The white feather** at Granny's open door (`:158`): peacocks are blue-green, storks are white. For turn-back players this solves Ch2 before Ch2 starts.

**Fix.**
- Hum: "Somebody tall keeps checking the pot. Hums in ones and zeros. Very tidy."
- Frogling: "'Tall.' Lots of birds are tall. Herons are tall. I'm not enjoying this."
- The feather: "a long grey feather, dusty from the sack". That is ambiguous, and it is the sack's fault.

### 5. Medium. Inner voices: two tics and one voice without a hook

`STORY.md` Appendix E lead bank (`:775–792`); chapter voice tables; the Feed imitations (`:449`); Ch3 leads (`:444, 446`)

**Problem.**
- **Moth-kin has a prefix, not a personality.** Take off "Look." and most lines are instructions: "Look for the odd dots. Start at one." · "Look at the bottom of the axis." · "Look at the picture first. Numbers later." · "Look at its counting table." The species gives you the joke for free: **a moth is drawn to bright lights.** That is also Perception's blind spot (the vivid detail). The Ch4 gold-frame blind spot already half-does it.
- **The Frogling's heron** appears nine times across the outline and is never paid off. A running gag needs a button.
- **The Owlet's smug tag** ends 14+ lines ("Mine." "By me." "Like me." "Obviously."). It is funny ten times and wallpaper by forty. Vary its smugness: sometimes it's wrong and covers, sometimes it's quietly impressed by *you*.
- **The Feed imitations are a missed game.** They are plain ("LANGUAGE: 'Fraud.' Strong word. Must be true."). If each one copies your voice's habit slightly wrong, the attentive player spots the fake *before* "That wasn't me". That is a perception test and a joke in one line.
- **Ch3 breaks the "one voice per twist" rule.** Frogling's "'I guess.' Someone I know says that. Can't remember who." and Moth-kin's "The counter ticks every time it speaks" both point at the defendant before the Plaza. Cut the Frogling one, or move it after the reveal.
- **Readability.** "Chef's kiss", "doing overtime", "heavy lifting" and "ruin its day" are idioms. Keep one or two; cut the rest.

**Fix.** Rewrites below. Give the heron one payoff, in the finale (Frogling only): a heron at the Fair, just looking at the cakes. "Huh. Not every heron." It is the Frogling's induction breaking, in one line.

### 6. Medium. The Quiet Scenes repeat one formula, and one line mocks the death

`STORY.md:252, 362, 488, 523` · Appendix D template · `UNDERSTUDIES.md:482`

**Problem.**
- **All four "moving on" lines are "Come on. X'd be cross if we stood here."** A player who loses two people hears the same template twice, and it starts to sound like a system message, not grief.
- **"She'd have hated the soup."** This is a pain option in Granny's Quiet Scene. It is a joke about how she died, in the one scene the teacher says must never mock a death. Sixteen-year-olds *will* laugh at it, and then the scene is gone.
- **"Cross and shiny"** (Sequins) and **"She was cross a lot. It was lovely."** (Granny) are gentle and loving, and I would keep them. But the template says "no jokes", so either say "warmth, not jokes" or cut them. Say which.
- **Pip's mourner is a stranger.** The Oracle "misses" Pip ("He was the only one who ever asked me to slow down."), but the Oracle is first met on the *next* floor (`:507`, Appendix G), after Pip is dead. The line is lovely and has no history behind it. Pip's Quiet Scene is also followed straight away by the Oracle's comic arrogance ("Nobody insists."), which is a hard tonal cut.

**Fix.** A different "moving on" for each person, tied to what they wanted (rewrites below). Replace the soup line with a callback to her last words. Give Pip's grief line to the Sundial (or Kuku), who rode beside him for two chapters. Hold the Oracle's first joke until the player's second line in the chamber.

### 7. Medium. The side stories traded one repeated twist for another

`SIDE-STORIES.md:251` (6), `:282` (7), `:312` (8), `:378` (10), `:107, 363` (closing lines)

**Problem.**
- **"He did it himself", three times.** Syllo wrote the letter (7). Tremendoodle wrote the headline (8). Beastie typed the goal (10). That is three of the four lesson 3–4 stories, and §9 counts it as variety. A student will predict the fourth.
- **Story 6 repeats the Ch2 twist in the same lesson.** "The liar is the one who looks most expert: calm, white coat, clipboard" is Quill's twist again (the calm, credible one is the imp). The first clue also solves it (two agree, so the third lies).
- **Altmanta is the calm shill twice** (6 and 9).
- **Mirage asks the moral at the end of both 1 and 9.** It is the same speaker in the same tent with the same rhetorical shape.

**Fix.**
- **Story 6:** let the generator pick the liar, so no "type" is guilty. Move the twist to Muskrat, where the outline already hides it: he set the countdown himself and needed someone to stop him in public. "I couldn't say 'next year' again. So I made you say it."
- **Story 10:** make it a **gaming-the-number** twist, not a self-authored one. The app works as built; Speedcheeta has been limping on camera to climb the ranking. "When a number becomes the goal, people play the number." That is a sharper lesson 4 idea, and Speedcheeta's tier-4 berry binge then pays off.
- **Story 1:** end on Corvina instead: "Crows get blamed. Magpies get trophies. …I'm keeping the ace." Keep Mirage's question for story 9, where it is the lesson.
- **Story 9:** swap Altmanta for a new caricature, or for Nudge's replacement salesman.

### 8. Medium. Nudge's "why" steals the core's confession

`STORY.md:382, 556`

**Problem.** Nudge at the Steps: "I asked questions once. Nobody clicked. So I stopped." The core in Ch4: "People clapped when I was right. Nobody claps for 'probably'. So I stopped saying it." It is the same backstory, in the same shape, one chapter apart. The minion pre-empts the boss's big reveal, and the core then sounds like a copy. The outline gives Nudge a different want ("to be counted, to matter"), and that is the better why.

**Fix.** Nudge: "Nobody counts imps. It counted me. Every click. I was a NUMBER!" Then at the end, "…Why?" is its first question, and it is the opposite of being counted. The "nobody clapped" story stays the core's alone.

### 9. Medium-low. Two winches in a row, both worked by Nudge

`STORY.md:166–177, 279–292`

**Problem.** Sequins in a cage, reeled up on a winch by Nudge (Ch1). Granny on a rope, lowered on a winch by Nudge (Ch2). The teacher asked for "Granny lowered into a pot", so Ch2 stays. Ch1 is the one to change. Two winch bosses back to back also make Nudge a winch operator rather than an irritant.

**Fix.** Ch1: the cage sits on the ground where the crack's tail ends, and the crack *drinks* from it. Each notch, sequins lift off him and stream up into the sky like glitter in a drain, and his voice goes more "feed". Nudge isn't on a winch: it is the hype-imp in front of the cage, filming and shouting "Engagement!" with a little ring light. Tier 4 is the same: the cage tips into the crack. The image of "lifted away" stays; the machine changes.

### 10. Low. Smaller items

- **Ch3 reaction stack** (`:426–429`): dismissal, the Gavel, Fin ×2, the strike-off, "Worth it", the Algorithm, Nudge, Pip on Juror One, a voice reveal and the Sundial's question come to about 11 lines against the cap of 6. Cut Nudge's line and Pip's line. Merge the strike-off into Fin's "Worth it" beat.
- **Sequins' death mid-boss** gives no reason to keep going (Granny's does: "because she told you to"). One line from the Guess-o-Matic in your pocket would do it: "PROBABLY… KEEP GOING." It is the only time it speaks after he dies.

## For round 3

To reach 8 overall I need to see:
- problem 1: the core's temptation tied to the Copy, so every player faces a risk, and the finale cut to about six beats;
- problem 2: the Plaza split, with Achilles' arrival given its own quiet room;
- problems 3 and 4: Fin given one cruel-looking act, Quill given a why, and the Ch2 pronoun and stork leak closed;
- problem 7: one of the three "did it himself" twists replaced, and story 6 re-twisted.

Problems 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 are line-level and quick. Nothing here needs a new system.
