## Remaining problems, by severity

### 1. Medium-high. For most players the climactic temptation is a one-button "No."

`STORY.md:531` (core beat 3)

**Problem.** The choice is "No." / "Let me hear it once more.", and the second option exists only "with any death". That is about 5–15% of players. Everyone else gets one button at the most important moment of the theme, so the refusal is a cutscene, not a decision. The upload clock gives the core real risk now, but the temptation itself costs nothing and offers nothing to the player who lost no one. Lesson 4 is about AI and the ethics of maths. The most tempting offer an algorithm makes a 16-year-old is not company. It is *"let me do it for you."*

**Fix.** Give every player a temptation that pays off in the game. The Copy offers to do the last trial's first step for you, perfectly. Accept, and the step is solved and the bar jumps 2 notches: it has learned it can replace you. Keep the sepia replay as an extra option when someone died. The refusal line stays as it is.

> **core:** IT CAN DO THE LAST ONE FOR YOU. PERFECTLY.
> **copy** *(your voice)*: Step aside. I'll answer. I'm always right.
> **Choice:** "No." / "Just this once." / *(with a death)* "Let me hear them once more."
> *(Just this once: the first step solves itself. Bar +2.)* **copy:** See? Nobody will notice.
> **avatar:** No. It's always right. That's how I'd know it isn't me.

### 2. Medium. The denouement says "it never knew you" three times, and "Mostly." steals "Probably."

`STORY.md:537–553` (what it is, the core lines, the voice, the Sundial's core question) · `:561` (finale beat 1)

**Problem.**
- From the trial 3 win to the end of the core there are about 16–18 lines: the tier line, the prediction, the `wonder` choice, the crack, the box, "PROBABLY.", one voice line, seven core lines, the plea, the `fate` choice, the last piece and the Sundial's question. Two core lines share one shape ("I called it knowing." / "I called it confidence."), and the opener "Oh. You looked behind the screens." only explains.
- **Asked and answered.** The core says "I never knew you. {n} data points." Then the Fox voice says "It didn't even know me", and the Sundial asks "Did it know you?" The student has heard the answer twice before the question arrives.
- **Finale beat 1** (Copy tiers 3–4) gives the avatar "Mostly." Two minutes later the game's last line is "Probably." The hedge is used up before the payoff.

**Fix.**
- Cut "Oh. You looked behind the screens." Merge the two "I called it" lines into one: *"{n} data points. A tally of your bad days. I called it knowing."*
- Drop the Sundial's question at the core. The `fate` choice *is* the question there. (Or replace it with one the scene hasn't answered: *"It said 'probably' once. Then it stopped. Will you?"*)
- Finale beat 1: let Granny identify you by your uncertainty, without you saying it.

> **granny** *(looking from one to the other)*: Are you sure you're {name}?
> **copy:** 100%.
> **granny:** That one's sure. So it isn't you. …That's my {name}.

### 3. Medium. The finale shelf is the new Nut Stall, and the `wonder` payoff fits one question in seven

`STORY.md:563`, Appendix F `:842`

**Problem.** One beat holds Granny's "Downhill" line, Sequins or Tally taking the toy, painting the sign, "I'd have sold it one day…", the `fate` line, the `wonder` line, Nudge's "…Why?", plus conditionals (Syllo, the tic ending, the heron, Mirage's label from story 9), and then the Sundial's "Will this one grow up loud?". That is 8–12 lines in one scene, just after the Copy beat and just before the last question. The `wonder` line ("You asked what it was before. Now we know.") answers only the Frogling's question. The other six (`Why do people believe you?`, `What's behind all that light?`…) get no payoff.

**Fix.**
- Make the conditionals **ambient**: unvoiced speech bubbles on the Fair map as you walk in (Syllo hoarse with his recruits, Sequins' tic ending, the heron for Frogling, Nudge asking passers-by "…Why?"). The shelf keeps only the toy, the sign, the `fate` line, the `wonder` line and "Will this one grow up loud?"
- Write all seven `wonder` answers:

> *(Why do people believe you?)* It sounded sure. That was all. Now it says "probably".
> *(What happens if nobody clicks?)* You asked what happens if nobody clicks. This. A quiet Fair.
> *(Owlet: premises)* You asked for its premises. Just one: "People clap for sure." False.
> *(Moth-kin: behind the light)* You asked what was behind the light. A toy. With one sequin.
> *(Fox: what if you're small?)* You asked what if it was small. It was. It still is.
> *(Frogling: before)* You asked what it was before. Now we know.
> *(Raven: "know")* You asked what it meant by "know". It meant "guess". The sign says so now.

### 4. Medium. Fin's turn line doesn't explain his plan, and the clear version sits in a table

`STORY.md:428, 471` (the turn) · `:56` (antagonist table)

**Problem.** "It had to say it in court. Then the shadow was evidence. And evidence goes home." A non-native 16-year-old can't follow it: saying "I guess" doesn't make the shadow evidence. Entering Exhibit A did that, and a dismissal sent it home. The line that makes it click, **"The vault never opens. Courts do."**, is in the antagonist table and nowhere in the script. A misjudged-villain reveal works only if the player understands the plan in one read.

**Fix** (tiers 1–3; replaces both Fin lines and the screens line, so the stack also gets back to six):

> **fin** *(quietly)*: The vault never opens. Courts do.
> **fin:** No confession, no trial. No trial, no Exhibit A. Sorry I made you say it.
> **fin** *(as the screens delete his licence)*: Licence gone. Worth it.

Tier 4: *"The vault never opens. Courts do. I bet with its life. I lost. I'm sorry."*

### 5. Medium. Both lesson 3 side stories turn on "the shouter wrote it himself"

`SIDE-STORIES.md:283` (7), `:313` (8); also `:167` (3)

**Problem.** Syllo wrote the recruitment letter (7). Tremendoodle wrote the headline (8). They are unlocked by the same trigger and played in the same lesson, so a student will predict story 8 from story 7. The round 3 note says they "teach different things", but the *twist* is the same shape. Story 7's twist is also weak on its own: a sergeant writing his own recruitment letter is normal, and its object clue gives it away. (Story 3's last line, "I have been eating my own pies", is a third self-authored ending, but there the common cause is the real twist, so it can stay.)

**Fix.** Keep story 8 (it is sharper satire) and re-twist story 7 on popularity itself. **The "everyone" was wooden.** The first eleven recruits are Syllo's own toy soldiers, painted as Fair folk. The real recruits joined because a crowd was already marching. The Raven's line ("'Everyone.' Count them. It's eleven.") now sets it up, and Syllo's loneliness stays.

> *Object clue:* the front rank never blinks. One has a paint chip on its nose.
> **usain volt:** I joined because everyone else did. …Is "everyone" made of wood?
> **syllo** *(quietly)*: They were the only ones who ever listened. I thought, if they marched, you'd follow.
> **syllo** *(last line)*: Fine. I'll shout at targets. Targets never leave.

### 7. Low. Two Feed imitations can't be spotted

`STORY.md:644–649` (Appendix B)

**Problem.** The rule is that each fake gets your voice's habit *slightly wrong*, so an attentive player spots it. The Owlet, Fox and Raven fakes do ("Elegant. Like everyone." / "Boring. Very realistic."). But PERCEPTION ("Look at the votes. Only the votes.") and MEMORY ("Rocks always lose trials… Every trial. All of them.") sound exactly like the real voices' own blind spots. The real Frogling says "Never has, never will." A Moth-kin or Frogling player gets a reveal they could not have seen coming.

**Fix.**
> **PERCEPTION (grey):** Look at the votes. Only the votes. Lights are boring.
> **MEMORY (grey):** Rocks always lose trials. I remember every one. Don't ask which.
