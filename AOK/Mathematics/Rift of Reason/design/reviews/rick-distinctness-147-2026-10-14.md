# Rick Ash-tley proposal distinctness — #147

14 October 2026. Base: 9f83a2882b81752d93f5ca72bb0e7f1e17138035. This is a design revision, not a runtime card release. The original card and persona are preserved in rick-distinctness-147-before.json.

The old cost-2, 2/1 Last Word returned itself to hand once per match, matching shipped Zuckerborg exactly. Keanu also uses that defeat hook with Guard. Colour, name and portrait did not make the proposal distinct.

Selected proposal, **Backstage Exit**: `Once per match, before an enemy spell targeting this resolves, return this to its owner's hand if there is room.` Rick slips backstage just as an unexpected spotlight finds him. The cost, body, Memory affinity, common rarity, mechanical/art IDs and pack allocation remain unchanged. The pose is a theatrical backward sidestep with one plain microphone. No lyrics, tune, copied performance, celebrity voice imitation or new persona is needed.

The audit covers shipped creatures, abilities, tactics, Axioms and the starter support pack, all 210 catalogue proposals, and the separate 23 conditional concepts. Zuckerborg/Keanu recover after defeat; Rethink/Unexpected Door are paid bounce actions; Replay Raccoon/Recall recover other cards from discard. This proposal consumes an enemy targeting opportunity while the creature is alive and preserves it for a later normally paid play. Creature combat, untargeted damage, silence and a full owner hand defeat the plan. Its low health is a real weakness. It has no Last Word recovery.

This is a new **pre-resolution** spell reaction. The separate conditional draft currently permits post-action reactions except the approved Guard interception. This proposal does not silently change that policy or claim that the current engine supports it: the catalogue adds an explicit `spell-reaction-policy` release gate alongside #109. #123 must settle and test this narrow visible-creature timing before shipping. The portrait can be produced for this distinct gated proposal. No conditional-system, save, grant, recording or runtime changes are part of #147.

Proposed implementation contract for that later release:

1. Qualify only after a legal enemy spell has committed its costs, card and action budget, but before its effects resolve. Enemy means an opponent of the creature's current controller. Spell means a personal Tactic/spell, not an Axiom, attack, creature ability or environmental event. No target-preview, cancelled choice or failed cast can consume the escape.
2. Consume one escape on the physical battle card's first qualifying cast, even if the owner's hand is full. Two owned copies can each use one escape; neither bounce, defeat, replay, control change nor silence removal resets it. Never allocate a new card identity to implement the return.
3. With room in the owner's hand, use normal bounce semantics: leave the current controller's board, reset normal transient board state and return to the owner. Replaying costs normal energy/actions and obeys sleeping/Swift rules. The match-use marker must survive the reset. With a full hand, keep the creature on board and resolve the spell normally; do not discard or defeat it as a substitute for escape.
4. Revalidate the spell's existing targets after the move. Do not retarget or refund it. Effects requiring that on-board creature fizzle; other legal targets and independent hero/draw effects resolve under their ordinary rules. Multiple qualifying Rick cards consume their own escape in stable target order, with hand space rechecked between moves.
5. Silenced/disabled Rick cannot trigger. Re-enabling the ability does not replay an earlier cast. Later spells act normally once the marker is consumed. Untargeted damage and lethal combat never cause the return.
6. Show the remaining escape to both players and explain full-hand/fizzle results in the existing action log. This is a visible printed passive; it neither arms a hidden card nor adds an optional priority window.

Uniqueness and exploit reasoning: one lifetime marker prevents replay/return loops; normal paid re-entry prevents free creatures/actions; no resource refund, card creation, attack reset or automatic-win interaction is added. Hand-capacity failure is finite and observable. Spell-cancellation ordering still needs runtime tests under its explicit policy gate. This document is a bounded proposal and counterplay audit, not measured balance or executable verification.
