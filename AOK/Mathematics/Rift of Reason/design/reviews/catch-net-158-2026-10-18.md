# First catch-game candidate (#158) — 18 October 2026

**Latest teacher direction: three distinct minigames, chosen randomly per catch, each scaled by rarity.** This supersedes the older single-mechanic restriction. This slice delivers only the first isolated candidate, terrain net cuts, plus cited research for the trio. It does not replace the campaign catch flow, provide three games, install rarity tuning or claim teacher acceptance. The other proposed candidates are Breakout-style ricochets and tiny Minesweeper-style deduction; see the repository research note.

## Playable candidate

`dev/catch-net.html` uses the new pure classic-script `Rift.CatchNetPrototype`. It never loads player saves, spends items, grants creatures, requests services or changes the campaign. Shipped Proof Pup, Comma Crab and Speedcheeta portraits are reused; no artwork/audio was generated.

A six-by-six arena has seven rocks and ten possible horizontal/vertical net cuts. Choose a net, inspect its ghost path, then cast. The creature takes up to three deterministic steps while the net is vulnerable. A crossing breaks that cast; otherwise the completed line partitions the room. Three casts are available, with a target of three squares (four for the net hunter). Completed space is shaded. This first prototype treats the whole selected line as vulnerable until resolution, rather than implementing continuous growth from two endpoints.

There are three behaviours **inside this one minigame**: steady movement, a right turn every third step, and an attack on an adjacent unfinished net at the first step. They are not the requested three separate minigames. Great Charm/lure each visibly add one cast in this prototype; both give five. These are local prototype settings, not changes to campaign item contracts.

Normal motion follows the committed path in finite steps and can be skipped. Calm/OS reduced motion commits immediately with the same visible path and result. All decisions use native buttons, including keyboard controls; no dragging or timing deadline is required. The default `first-catch-2` is a friendly demonstration: Horizontal 1 then Vertical 3 catches it. New arena supplies varied seeds. Replay seed supports repeatable comparison.

## Solvability and measured baseline

Generation admits a layout only after finite memoized search finds a legal winning sequence under the **unassisted** budget. A bounded admission loop has a separately verified deterministic fallback. Search includes every legal cast, including a broken cast that moves the creature; it does not claim every winning route is safe or obvious to a one-action player. An assisted game uses the same layout with extra casts.

The frozen `one-action-v1` baseline sees the same selected-cast previews as the player. It prefers safe cuts, then the smallest resulting room; seed-fixed candidate order breaks ties. It has no future search or hidden RNG. Calibration used `calibration:` seeds; the report uses separate `held-out-catch-158:` seeds.

See the adjacent JSON receipt. Run `node tools/sim-catch-net.mjs --runs=1000 --output=<report.json>` from the game folder.

These are bot/solver measurements for one prototype configuration. They are **not** student success rates, rarity-specific balance, measured 20–60-second playtimes or evidence of fun. A teacher/class playtest must establish those separately, and each of the eventual three games needs its own evidence.
