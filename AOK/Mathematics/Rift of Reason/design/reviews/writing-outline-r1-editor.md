# Writing gate, outline round 1: Critic 3, the editor

Date: 8 October 2026. Documents: `design/STORY.md`, `design/SIDE-STORIES.md`, `design/UNDERSTUDIES.md`. I judged them against `design/TEACHER-STORY-NOTES.md`, including items 4–7, which arrived after the outline was written. Scale: 10 is a story I'd fight to publish, 7 is competent but forgettable, 5 is flat.

## Scores

| Document | Score | In one line |
|---|---|---|
| **Overall** | **6 / 10** | A strong premise and theme, and the best twist I have read in a classroom game. But the antagonists don't recur, the hero wants nothing, the player makes no real choice, and grief is played as a rescue. |

None passes the gate (8). The fixes are structural but cheap: most of them re-use characters and beats that already exist.

## Skim test

**Method.** First I read only the headings and the first line of each section. Then I read all three documents properly.

**STORY.md: clear, but not compelling on a skim.**
- Before the first scene there are 316 lines of machinery: a fixes table, world rules, "How the text works now", stakes clocks, death rules, the inner-voice lead bank.
- §1 "The spine" is the exception, and it is excellent. In five sub-headings ("What the Algorithm wants", "Why you", "What is at stake", "The four twists", "How it ends") a reader gets the whole story and wants it.
- The chapters then open with "**Arc.** Informative start: …". That is critic language, not story. The beat headings are station names ("Signpost.", "Forest Road.", "Troll Bridge."), so on a skim each chapter reads like a map walkthrough.
- The dramatic beats (Granny taken, the pot, the defendant, the core) sit at beat 9 or 11, or inside tables of clues.
- Verdict: **the story is clear from §1 alone, and the chapters do not pull a skimmer forward.**

**SIDE-STORIES.md: passes.**
- The teasers work as one-line hooks: "Shouting from the Witness Tent. The door is tied shut." / "The drawbridge is half up. Something in a top hat is hanging from it."
- On a skim, though, the sameness shows. Two barricade negotiations, two "crowd blames the dark animal" stories, and a run of twists that resolve to "an imp or the feed did it".

**UNDERSTUDIES.md: fails as story.**
- It reads as a production document (roster, voice casting, art budget), which is fair for its purpose.
- The only story content a skimmer meets is the takeover gag and the memorial template, and both are tonally wrong for note 4 (problem 2).

## Editorial letter

The premise is the best thing here, and it is genuinely good. A prediction feed wants 100% certainty, so it tries to switch off thinking where thinking starts. That turns the TOK idea into a villain's motive. The game's second line, "On cloudy days I guess", is a theme a reader can hold in one hand: the Sundial guesses and says so; the Algorithm guesses and calls it knowing. The Ch4 reveal (the colossus is Sequins' little Guess-o-Matic, grown loud because "nobody claps for 'probably'") is fair, set up, funny and sad. I would keep it at any cost.

**The protagonist.** What the story does not have yet is a protagonist. "Why you" explains why the *Algorithm* wants the avatar, not what the avatar wants. The avatar is a dry observer who follows the trail, solves the station and makes a flavour choice. Nothing in the prologue gives them a want or a flaw, so nothing at the core can feel like change. An arc is hiding in the design:
- In Ch1 every inner voice is confidently wrong.
- In Ch4 the Algorithm reads out "YOU FOLLOW YOUR VOICE 78%".
- At the core the voice falls silent and you think alone.

That is the story of someone who trusts their own certainty and learns to check it and say "probably". The writer should make it the spine of the avatar, on purpose.

**The antagonists.** The Algorithm talks a lot but never stands in your way. It is a caps-lock voice that loses only through its proxies, and it reacts to each lost shadow piece with one line. Each chapter brings a new villain, and none of them is seen again:
- Miss Quill exists for one chapter and ends in a jar.
- Fin and Speedcheeta exist for one chapter.
- The imps have no faces and no names.

This is the "villain of the week" pattern the teacher has now ruled out (note 5).

**The emotional line.** It is well designed on paper: a friend, then family, then your companion, then yourself. In practice it barely fires:
- Deaths are tuned to about 5% of players, and only for those who saw every earlier scene.
- So about 95% of students finish with "a bit bald, smells of leek".
- The top rung, "yourself", is the most comic peril in the game: a Copy that says "like and subscribe".
- When a death does happen, the next chapter treats it as a small rescue (take back the shawl, scatter the imps), which the teacher has now forbidden (note 4).

**The ending.** It is thematically perfect and dramatically soft. The machine asks to stay small and "you bring it home". The player decides nothing about the antagonist they spent four lessons fighting, while side story 9 hands them exactly that decision (switch it off / label it / leave it) about a lesser machine.

**What to do.** Give the hero a want and a last line, give the villains faces that come back, let the player turn round, and let loss be quiet. Then this is an 8.

## Biggest problems (by severity)

### 1. The antagonists don't recur, and the minions have no faces (note 5)
**Where:** `STORY.md` §1, §3 (the Algorithm's ladder), Ch2 Town Hall win, Ch3, Ch4; `SIDE-STORIES.md` stories 2, 3 and 8 (three separate nameless imps).

**Problem:**
- Every chapter boss is new and disposable. Quill is jarred and never seen again. Fin, Speedcheeta and the Copy each live in one chapter.
- The Algorithm is a voice, and its imps are interchangeable.
- Nobody mocks you on the Road, gets in your way, flees or comes back.

**Why it matters:**
- A reader's payoff comes from the third meeting with a villain, not the first.
- Without recurrence the final showdown has no history to discharge. The core reveal is clever but not cathartic, because we never fought *it*.

**Fix:**
- **Create one named recurring imp lieutenant** (working name: **Nudge**). Nudge holds the sack when the shadow is stolen, is the well imp (SS2), wears the cat mask (SS3), runs Quill's winch (Ch2), runs the auto-headline machine and counts the GUILTY vote (Ch3, SS8), and builds the Copy (Ch4).
  - Each time, Nudge mocks you, gets beaten, and loses something visible: its sack, its mask, its tail-bell, its follower count.
  - We learn why in Ch3: Nudge was "noise" once, a curious imp who asked questions, until the feed told it that questions don't get clicks. That gives the core a second redemption: Nudge's choice.
- **Quill escapes the jar** in the Ch2 aftermath. She loses her mask but runs. She returns in Ch3 in a new mask as the juror who starts the "#GUILTY" vote, and at the core as the Algorithm's last believer. Her "Tomorrow is waiting" line already sets this up.
- **The Algorithm loses something each chapter that the player can see.** Each shadow piece it loses makes it slip once into lower-case "probably" by accident, which it angrily deletes. This mirrors the Sundial getting surer, and it fairly clues the Ch4 twist.
- **Vary how bosses leave** (note 5: "not the same pattern"): Ch1 the imp flees with half the sack; Ch2 Quill escapes; Ch3 the "villain" (Fin) turns out to be an ally (problem 6); Ch4 the Algorithm begs.

### 2. Death is followed by a rescue and a gag, not a solemn scene (note 4)
**Where:** `STORY.md` §4 ("Too late (lethal)"), §5 "What changes after each possible death", Ch3 Plaza beat 4; `UNDERSTUDIES.md` §4 tone rule 2, §4.0, §4.1; `SIDE-STORIES.md` story 6, tier 4.

**Problem:** the "scene that always succeeds" is a mini-rescue.
- After Sequins dies, his collection is sewn onto the Mayor's coat ("New look! The imps did it.").
- After Granny dies, the imps stream her pot as "SOUP OF THE FUTURE — LIVE" and you scatter them. This is grotesque, and it is framed as a win.
- The memorial is a skippable drop-in "the next time you pass".
- The takeover gag (§4.0) plays *before* the memorial (§4.1).
- The memorial includes a caricature joke guest ("Tremendous funeral. The best."), "There were sandwiches", and a Feed −1 reward for grieving.
- The Mayor's death leaves a statue "mostly of his hat".

**Why it matters:** the teacher has said exactly what is wanted. Beyond that, readers forgive a story that kills a character. They do not forgive a story that winks at the death. One wink teaches 16-year-olds that the loss was content.

**Fix:** one mandatory **Quiet Scene** that **opens the next chapter**.
- **Where it plays:**
  - Sequins dies → Ch2 Stone Circle.
  - Granny dies → Ch3 Rift Landing.
  - The Sundial dies → Ch4 Tower Door, not mid-chapter at the Stairwell.
  - Side-story deaths → the first scene after the story.
- **Rules:**
  - It is not skippable, 6–8 lines long, and has no reward.
  - It never makes the player win anything back.
- **Five beats:**
  1. **Their things.** You carry what was left: the cage cushion, the boiled shawl, the silent dial. The avatar *brings* it; nobody fights for it.
  2. **Last words.** Each lethal tier needs real last words, replayed here: Sequins' "Oh. It's… shiny in there." works; Granny needs one, and UNDERSTUDIES' "Under-seasoned. Tell them I said so." is good.
  3. **Who misses them.** One named friend, one sincere line.
  4. **Pain.** The avatar's line is a choice of two plus silence ("…"). The Hum Charm stays silent when you hum.
  5. **Moving on.** The Sundial or Kuku: "Come on. They'd be cross if we stood here."
- **After it:**
  - The understudy arrives in a *later* scene, and the warm comedy can start there.
  - The villain may be callous inside the Quiet Scene ("SAD CONTENT PERFORMS WELL" is fine; it is the Algorithm talking).
  - Cut the hat statue, the funeral jokes, the sandwiches and the Feed reward.
  - Cut the "SOUP OF THE FUTURE" stream entirely, or keep it as an Algorithm boast the player can only turn off.

### 3. All four bosses use the same template
**Where:** `STORY.md` §4 and the four peril sections.

**Problem:** every boss runs the same pattern:
- an 8-notch clock;
- three puzzle stages;
- a "scripted push" warned one line earlier;
- a "climax beat" where you pick the right line (Ch2 the sentence, Ch3 the record line, Ch4 the question);
- four tiers.

**Why it matters:**
- Note 5 says "not the same pattern every lesson".
- Pacing: by Ch3 a student can feel the rhythm, the dread goes, and the clock becomes UI.
- A climax the reader can predict is not a climax.

**Fix:** keep the widget and change the shape.
- **Ch1, a trap:** the Gate is a deduction you can win *and still lose the piece*. The imp flees with it if Danger is over half, and you get it back at the Rift Pass.
- **Ch2, a farce with a choice:** the Mayor branch (problem 4). The climax is a dinner-table accusation scene, not a fourth puzzle.
- **Ch3, a trial:** keep the trial; it is the right shape here.
- **Ch4, no clock at the end:** the core is a conversation. The player decides the Algorithm's fate (problem 4c).

### 4. The player has no real choice, and the story never turns back (note 7)
**Where:** `STORY.md` §6 avatar-only options, Ch1 twist clues, Ch2 suspicion choice, the core and finale; `SIDE-STORIES.md` story 9.

**Problem:**
- `brave`, `cover`, `suspect` and `wonder` are flavour. Each changes one or two lines and never changes where you go or whom you confront.
- Ch1 lays five clues that Granny is in danger, then gives a careful player no way to act on them.
- The core decides the Algorithm's fate for you.

**Why it matters:**
- A twist the reader saw coming and could not act on feels like a railroad.
- A choice that costs nothing is not a choice.

**Fix:**
- **(a) Ch1, turn back.** After Hum 2, the Card Sharp and the Campfire offer "Go back to the Fair."
  - You cannot save Granny. That is the twist, and it stays.
  - You do find her cottage door open, a trail of soup-pot imps, and Quill's chalk 0 1 0 1 on the gate.
  - You enter Ch2 already knowing the kitchen and the stork. This is a different reveal order, not a different plot.
  - The cost: Sequins' clock starts 1 notch higher, because you were away.
- **(b) Ch2, confront the Mayor (the teacher's own example).**
  - Layer the Mayor: the table rightly proves he is **not an imp**, but he **signed the Feast of Laws over to Quill** for applause and endless bread. He is driving the town toward disaster.
  - At the Town Hall Stairs, Granny hums "come up". You may instead turn back to the Square and confront him. This is a negotiation, and it gets the Feast decree torn up (the imps lose their cover) or the kitchen key.
  - Cost: +1 notch on Granny's rope.
  - TOK payoff: "The table answered the question you asked. Not the one you should have asked."
  - This keeps the red-herring lesson and adds a real moral turn.
- **(c) The finale, the player decides.** At the core: switch it off / take it home / label it "IT GUESSES" and leave it running in Tomorrowton.
  - Each choice changes the shelf scene and the Sundial's last question.
  - Move side story 9's identical choice (switch off / label / leave) so it becomes the *rehearsal* for this one, or give story 9 a different decision.

### 5. The protagonist wants nothing and does not change
**Where:** `STORY.md` §1 "Why you", the prologue beats, the finale.

**Problem:**
- The avatar's want is borrowed from others (find Granny, get the shadow back).
- The avatar's voice is fixed ("dry and a little cheeky") from first line to last.
- The game's closing question, "Which one are you?", is never answered by the hero.

**Why it matters:** a story is a want under pressure that ends in change. Without one, the player is a tourist through good scenery. The editor's question, "do we care what happens to *you*?", gets no answer, and that is why the Ch4 "the risk is yourself" rung does not land.

**Fix:** use the arc that is already latent in the inner voice.
- **Prologue: give the avatar a certain, cocky want.**
  - For example, they want to win the Fair's thinking trophy and are sure they will ("I always know the answer").
  - Granny teases them: "Always? Even the Sundial guesses."
- **Middle: let the voice be wrong.** Ch1 shows it (all five blind spots). In Ch3 the Feed voice imitates it. In Ch4 the readout shows "YOU FOLLOW YOUR VOICE 78%".
- **Ending: the change, in one word.** The avatar's last line answers the Sundial: "Probably." The Sundial (or Kuku): "Good. Say it like that."
- That is the theme, embodied, in one word.

### 6. No misjudged villain (note 6)
**Where:** `STORY.md` Ch2 (the Mayor) and Ch3 (Fin).

**Problem:** the Mayor is a comic red herring (a bread thief), not someone vilified for a hard choice. Nobody in the story pays a short-term cost for a long-term good.

**Why it matters:**
- It is the teacher's direction.
- It is also the strongest TOK-ethics beat available.
- For a reader, a reversal of moral judgement hits harder than a reversal of identity.

**Fix: make Prosecutor Fin the misjudged villain.**
- **What he did:**
  - Fin brought the case against the Sundial on purpose.
  - A trial is the only lawful way to force the Algorithm to hand Exhibit A (the shadow piece) out of its vault and into a court, where a dismissed case must return evidence to its owner.
  - He risked your friend for the piece. The player hates him all chapter; the Fox blind spot ("The fraud is Fin… classic secret villain") already points this way, so turn it into a double bluff.
- **Clues:**
  - He asks about your shadow.
  - He insists Exhibit A be "formally entered".
  - His "first loss" was chosen: "I have never lost a case I wanted to win."
- **Closing question:** the Sundial asks "Was he right to risk me?"
- **Cost:** Fin loses his licence (the Algorithm's vote strikes him off), so the good act really costs him.
- **Alternatives:** Corvina (SS1) is a small version already. The layered Mayor (problem 4b) is a "misjudged in the other direction" piece.

### 7. Loss never reaches most players, and the final peril is the softest
**Where:** `STORY.md` §4 tuning target, §5 rules 7–8, Ch4 "The peril: the Copy of you".

**Problem:**
- Deaths are tuned to about 5% each, and armed only for players who saw every earlier scene.
- A classroom jump-in student can never lose anyone.
- The "yourself" rung is a comic Copy that charms Granny into subscribing.

**Why it matters:** the emotional throughline is the editor's sixth test. For 9 in 10 readers the story has no cost and an ending without weight.

**Fix:**
- **One guaranteed, unpreventable cost for everyone.** The best candidate is the misjudged villain's sacrifice (Fin struck off, problem 6). An alternative: whatever the tier, the Sundial gets back only three of its four pieces, and is "a little cloudy, always" from then on.
- **Make the Copy threaten relationships, not slapstick.**
  - The Copy goes home *to Granny and the Sundial*, and they cannot tell the difference.
  - Its warning lines are your own kind words to them (the Ch1 comfort line, your `brave` answer) in the wrong mouth.
  - Tier 4: Granny greets it as you.

### 8. Sequins is put in mortal danger before we care about him
**Where:** `STORY.md` §7 beat 3, §8 beats 4 and 8.

**Problem:**
- Two short scenes (the Pattern Stall, fishing at the Well), then he is caged at the first boss and may die.
- The Algorithm's motive for caging its own maker ("YOU TAUGHT ME MY FIRST NUMBER") is the richest seam in the story, and it is never paid off.

**Why it matters:**
- You cannot grieve a stranger. The outline says this itself in §5 rule 7, then breaks it in spirit.
- The core reveal would double in force if the reader knew *why* the child caged the father.

**Fix:**
- **Give Sequins one warm private beat** with the avatar at the stall. He shows you the Guess-o-Matic as "my little apprentice; it's honest, it says probably", and asks you to mind it if anything happens.
- **Answer the motive at the core:** "He taught me 'probably'. I wanted to stop him teaching it to me." The trap was a time-loop attempt to un-teach itself.
- **If `dead:sequins`,** the core's "Is he… here?" then lands as the saddest line in the game.

### 9. The villain's tactics are too abstract for a teen reader
**Where:** `STORY.md` §1 "What the Algorithm wants", Ch2.

**Problem:** why boil *Granny*? "Boil away the first rules of logic" works for a designer who knows she is Granny *Axiom*. A 16-year-old non-native reader will not join "axiom soup" up without help.

**Why it matters:** a reader who doesn't understand a threat feels no threat.

**Fix:** one line, from Quill or an imp, at the Square or the pot: "Granny Axiom keeps the first rules. Boil the keeper, and the rules go soft. Everything after wobbles." Say it plainly once.

### 10. The lesson 1 side stories duplicate each other, and the twists repeat
**Where:** `SIDE-STORIES.md` stories 1 and 3 (also 1 and 8; 2, 3, 7, 8 and 10).

**Problem:**
- Stories 1 and 3 have the same shape: the crowd blames the dark-feathered or dark-furred one, and it was a *different* black bird or cat. Keanu Meows is a key witness in both.
- Stories 1 and 8 are both barricade negotiations ("locked from inside").
- Five of the ten twists are "an imp or the feed did it" (2, 3, 7, 8, 10). That absolves every person and quietly works against note 6.

**Why it matters:** these are one-shots, so each must feel new on a skim. Repetition teaches students to guess the twist from the genre instead of reasoning.

**Fix:**
- **Re-twist story 3:** there is no thief. Rawmsay sleep-bakes and sleep-eats, and the cat walks by because the oven timer rings at the same time each day: a correlation with a common cause. That is pure induction-vs-deduction, and no imp.
- **Story 8:** make it a public debate the player must referee, not a siege.
- **Let at least three twists turn on a human choice:**
  - Beastie *did* type GOAL = VIEWS, and is ashamed.
  - Syllo wrote his own letter.
  - Siuuugull knows the well is fake and keeps wishing because it calms him.
- **Use the recurring imp (problem 1) in at most three stories,** so its appearances are events.

### 11. Optional one-shots carry deaths, including a joke death
**Where:** `SIDE-STORIES.md` story 6, tier 4 (the Mayor) and story 7, tier 4 (Syllo); `STORY.md` §5 table.

**Problem:**
- A loved Fair character (Syllo) can die in a 5–10 minute optional story that note 8 says is "not main plot".
- A comic minor character drowns, and his memorial is a hat statue.
- Note 4's solemn next-chapter scene has no natural home for a side-story death.

**Why it matters:** a death in optional content reads as either throwaway or a punishment for curiosity. The hat statue breaks note 4 outright.

**Fix:**
- **Remove the Mayor's lethal tier.** He is needed alive for the problem 4b branch anyway.
- **Turn Syllo's tier 4 into a recurring-antagonist hook:**
  - He marches into the feed and comes back **as one of its minions**: loud, recruiting for the Algorithm.
  - You face him in Ch4 (the Sky Bridge Champion, or a floor guard).
  - You can bring him back with the sound argument he refused in SS7.
  - That is loss, recurrence and payoff in one beat.

### 12. The prologue starts slowly, and the Ch3 middle sags
**Where:** `STORY.md` §7 (beats 1–5 before the crack), §10 beat 2 ("Pip hosts. Each win is Pip reacting, happily filing").

**Problem:**
- **Prologue:** four stalls of pleasant setup before the inciting incident. In a lesson with a time limit, some students will leave before the sky splits.
- **Ch3:** four stations in a row where nothing happens except Pip filing.

**Why it matters:** momentum. The first scene's hook is a talking sundial being charming, and the hook is the crack.

**Fix:**
- **Prologue:** cold-open with two lines from the eye in the "hair on the sky": "NOISE DETECTED. FAIR, 9:02. SUBJECT: {name}." Then cut to the Sundial waking you. The player knows from line 1 that they are being watched.
- **Ch3:** dramatic irony. Each Pip station you win makes the Plaza GUILTY counter tick up on the HUD, "evidence filed", before the player knows who the defendant is. When the Steps twist lands, the player realises they built it. That turns four filler stations into the chapter's dread.

## Suggestions for the teacher's new direction (notes 4–7)

**Recurring antagonists and minions (note 5)**
- **Nudge the imp** (problem 1) is the recurring minion across every chapter and three side stories. It mocks, steals, loses a thing each time, and gets a "why" in Ch3 and a choice at the core.
- **Quill** escapes the jar, maskless. She returns in Ch3 as the vote-starter and at the core as the last believer, and gets a final face-off line: "No maybes." / Avatar: "Probably."
- **Speedcheeta** films every defeat of yours (the Fair crowd, SS1, the Steps, SS10). He is a rival irritant whose "why" is that nobody ever watched him until the feed did. The clip negotiation becomes his turn.
- **The Algorithm** loses something you can see with each piece: a "probably" slips out, then a lower-case word, then its eye shrinks.
- **Not the same pattern every lesson:** one flees, one escapes, one is revealed as an ally (Fin), one begs.

**Solemn aftermath of deaths (note 4)**
- Use the Quiet Scene template from problem 2. It opens the next chapter, cannot be skipped, has no reward, and never makes you win anything back.
- **Order:** the Quiet Scene, then later the understudy's arrival (where warmth and comedy may return), then the memorial candle at the Fair.
- Rewrite UNDERSTUDIES §4 tone rule 2 to: "**Grief first, in the story's own voice, with no jokes. Warmth later, from the living, in a later scene. The villain may be callous; the narrator never is.**"

**A misjudged "villain" (note 6)**
- **First choice:** Prosecutor Fin (problem 6). He risked the Sundial to force the shadow piece into court, and is struck off for it.
- **Second:** the layered Mayor (problem 4b) is the reverse case: shown as a harmless clown, but morally responsible.
- **Small-scale ones in side stories:** Corvina (SS1, already close), Beastie (SS10, if he typed the goal himself and owns it).

**Real choice and non-linear reveals (note 7)**
- Ch1 "turn back to the Fair" (problem 4a).
- Ch2 "turn back and confront the Mayor" (problem 4b, the teacher's example).
- **Ch3 "defend or bargain":** before the trial, the Algorithm offers to drop the case if you hand over the two pieces you hold. If you accept, the Sundial lives but Ch4's clock starts 2 higher and the Sundial knows what you did. If you refuse, you face the trial. This is a real dilemma, and the story bends round it.
- The finale's fate of the core (problem 4c).
- Every branch rejoins the linear map, so the game stays linear and the story goes back and forth.

## What to keep (don't lose these in the rewrite)

- "On cloudy days I guess." The spine, the theme and the last question.
- The Guess-o-Matic twist, and "Nobody claps for 'probably'."
- Quill and the liar sentence: "True. No. False. No. ONE. ZER—"
- The Sundial on trial for guessing, Pip's arc from recording to checking, and Fin's "I will need to check my premises."
- The hum ladder ("I am not a carrot person"), "Is it soup yet? / Tradition. / Me, dear. I'm in it.", and "SHADOW SAVED TO FAVOURITES".
- Kuku: a clock that is never uncertain replacing the honest guesser. This is the best understudy idea; it deserves the main story's attention.
- Sequins' last words: "Oh. It's… shiny in there."
