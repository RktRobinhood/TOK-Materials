# Colour-scheduled Fate spells and Fate timing rules: simulation (#121)

17 October 2026. This measures the five colour-scheduled spells (Prepared Defence, Delayed Observation, Tomorrow's Idea, Remember This, Scheduled Speech) and the four Fate timing Axioms (Patient Plans, Hasty Plans, Open Calendar, Gentle Consequences), as implemented in #121. They are bench and tests only, so nothing here is a claim about real students. It is the AI playing against itself, used to catch a card that is far too strong or too weak before release.

## Method

`tools/sim-fate-pack.mjs` uses the real engine, AI and starter decks (data/starters.js).

```
node tools/sim-fate-pack.mjs --games=60 --workers=3
node tools/sim-fate-pack.mjs --games=60 --workers=3 --only=spells --patch=prepared-defence.cost:1,delayed-observation.cost:1,tomorrows-idea.cost:1,remember-this.cost:1
```

- **Spells.** Each spell goes into the starter of its colour, in place of that deck's generic pair of the same cost (2). The deck plays all five starters, itself included, in both seats, 60 seeds each. Each game is played twice with the same seed: once with the unchanged deck and once with the spell. That gives 600 paired games per spell, and the 95% interval is from the paired differences.
- **Timing rules.** The five decks, each with its spell pair, play each other. One side's ten Axioms swap Small Assumptions and Growing Ideas for two copies of the rule, so the rule is in the shared deck: 600 games per rule.
- **Settings.** Both sides play at Competent, and there are no hero powers (those are measured in avatar-powers-balance.md). The game options are the defaults.

## What this says

- **In AI play, the spells are weaker than the cards they replace, and cost is not the reason.** At cost 1 the losses shrink by only 1 to 2 points. What limits these decks is the two-cards-a-turn budget and the round of waiting, not energy. So the draft costs (2) stay. Halving them would make the cards look cheap without making them work.
- **The baselines are strong cards.** Counterexample (3 damage for 2) and Pep Talk (+2/+2) are among the best generic cards, so a slow effect that replaces them loses ground. Against Second Wind, Scheduled Speech is level, and Remember This is close even though the AI almost never casts it.
- **The AI under-uses two of them.** It casts Delayed Observation in only a quarter to a third of games, and Remember This in 4 to 5%. Its look-ahead values a bigger hand weakly, and Remember This needs a defeated creature in the discard pile. So these numbers partly measure the AI, not the card. A playtest with students would show more.
- **Timing rules: the AI never plays them** (0 plays in 2,400 games). Its rule scoring sees no change on the board from a rule that only moves future spells. They still come into play through Fate flips (about 0.14 per game), so the engine and screen paths get exercised. This is a limit of the AI opponent, not of the rules, and it matters only once students can own them.

## Recommendation for the release decision

Keep the pack bench-only, as it is now. Before granting it, decide in a playtest whether the slow spells should do more rather than cost less. For example: a 2/2 Idea, Prepared Defence giving +1/+1, Delayed Observation drawing 3, or Remember This letting you choose the creature. Then run this tool again with the changed numbers. Unlock pacing can't be measured until a reward slot is chosen; `tools/sim-rewards.mjs` measures the cadence once one is.

Aside, outside #121: without hero powers, the unchanged starters range from 26% (raven) to 77% (owlet) against the field at Competent. The starter balance target (45 to 55%) is measured with powers, which this run leaves out.
