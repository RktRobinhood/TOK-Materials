## The whole arc, briefly

The spine is now right. You lose something at the Fair. Then you lose a friend (Sequins), family (Granny), your companion (the Sundial), and finally your *self* (the Copy). Each chapter has an informative start, a Hum Charm or vote counter that builds risk, a stakes-clock climax and a payoff. "On cloudy days I guess" as the seed of the whole theme is a genuinely good piece of design: the second line of the game becomes the charge at the trial and the last question of the game.

Three things bend the curve the wrong way.

- **The peak comes too early.** The two deadliest perils are in Ch2 (Granny) and Ch3 (the Sundial). The final climax is a non-lethal Copy whose worst outcome is "She subscribed." The game's last 40 minutes have the lowest stakes in it.
- **Every twist is announced.** The outline writes "fair" as "five to seven clues, plus a voice lead per species". A careful 16-year-old solves Quill in the Square, the defendant at the Plaza and the Guess-o-Matic at the Oracle. A twist is fair with two or three clues that are ambiguous when they appear.
- **The peril shape repeats.** Sequins hangs from a winch (Ch1), Granny hangs from a rope (Ch2), and the Mayor hangs from a chain (side story 6, Ch2). That is three danglings in two lessons.

## The biggest problems, by severity

### 1. Every twist is over-clued, and the inner voice gives the answer away

`STORY.md:572–579, 607–613, 588` (Ch2) · `:678, 691–697` (Ch3) · `:813, 900, 857` (Ch4) · `:422, 483–484` (Ch1)

**Problem.**
- **Ch2.** Quill gets seven listed clues. Her first line, "No maybes in my classroom", is a villain line. Her win line is described as "creepy-warm". On top of that, **all five** species' story leads point at her: Raven's says "Nobody bans a word they aren't afraid of", and Raven's suspicion option names her outright. "The calm one did it" is also a trope that 16–17-year-olds know well.
- **Ch3.** The Plaza poster shows "a silhouette of something round with a stick", which is a picture of a sundial. Fin asks "Nice shadow. Do you always travel with it?"
- **Ch4.** The Oracle says the twist in plain words one floor before the core: "The Algorithm was small once. It said 'probably', like me."
- **Ch1.** Hum 2 ends with Granny cut off mid-word ("Just let me get the—"). The player hears the kidnapping halfway through the chapter. The Gate then only reveals that it was planned. This also contradicts the outline's own rule 4: "Its silence is the Ch1 clue."

**Why it matters.** Criterion 3 asks for a turn that changes how you see what came before. A reveal the player already worked out lands as a confirmation, which is exactly the baseline's complaint about the Mayor. The voice leads make it worse: the game whispers the answer in the player's own head.

**Fix.** Cap each twist at **three** clues. Each should be ambiguous when it appears, and at least one should be hidden inside a joke. The Mayor's "Also, this statement is false" tic and Quill's flinch are the model.
- **Make Quill likeable and useful.** She should save you from the Constable at the Post Office by vouching for your cover story. Then the twist hurts, and it doubles the lesson: her vouching was a "consistent, checked" story too (rewrite below).
- **Turn story leads into observations without conclusions.** Moth-kin's "Her scarf doesn't move. There's wind." is right. Raven's and Owlet's leads draw conclusions. Change Raven's suspicion option so it does not name Quill.
- **Ch3:** cut the silhouette and Fin's shadow line. Keep "FRAUD ADMITS: 'I GUESS'", which is a perfect, fair, buried callback.
- **Ch4:** cut the Oracle's "it said 'probably', like me". Keep the Gate line "PROFESSOR. YOU TAUGHT ME MY FIRST NUMBER", which is eerie and early.
- **Ch1:** Hum 2 should end *normally*, and the dread should come from the silence after it (rewrite below).

### 2. The final climax has the smallest stakes in the game

`STORY.md:73–76, 819–835`

**Problem.** The outline says "the risk climbs… then yourself". But the Copy can't hurt anyone, and its tier 4 is a gag ("It said 'like and subscribe' to Granny. She subscribed."). A player who lost Granny or the Sundial reaches a finale that never touches that loss.

**Why it matters.** Criterion 2 says the whole game should build to its climax. Right now it peaks at the pot and the vote. The understudy system, the most emotional thing the outline adds, gets no payoff at the end.

**Fix.** Give the core one temptation, in the spirit of Undertale.
- **If anyone has died:** the Algorithm offers them back as a perfect prediction. "Everything she usually said. 100% accurate." The fake Granny speaks **only in her own earlier recorded lines**, replayed. That costs no new voice lines and is the whole TOK point of lesson 4: a model of past data is not the person. You refuse, or you accept and hear it loop, then refuse.
- **If nobody died:** offer back the Sundial's lost certainty, or let the Copy target the people you saved ("They'll never notice. I say what you say."). Its tier 4 should cost something real: Granny doesn't recognise you for one scene of the finale. That is a short scene that always succeeds, and it isn't a joke.

### 3. The inner voices are hints with a keyword, not personalities

`STORY.md:265–295` (lead bank), and the story-lead tables in every chapter

**Problem.** Remove the labels and most leads are tutorial instructions: "Shade the 'no' parts first. Then place the 'some'." · "Follow one wire at a time to the bulb." · "Keep a record: who accused whom." · "Count lines at each dot." You can only tell the species apart by a verb ("Picture…" is Fox, a quoted word is Raven, "Remember/last time" is Frogling). None of the 75 leads is a joke. The §6 personality column says the Owlet is "pleased with itself" and the Frogling is "deadpan", but the lines show neither.

Some blind spots are not seductive, just stupid. Raven's "'Fine.' She said fine. That settles it." comes right after Granny is cut off mid-word, and no player will believe it. Disco Elysium's skills are dangerous because they are persuasive and fun to listen to.

**Why it matters.** The brief says "five inner voices with personalities". These lines will be read about 60 times per playthrough. If they feel like a hint button, players will skip them, and then the blind spots and the Ch4 "you follow your voice 78%" payoff have nothing to land on.

**Fix.** Give each voice a fixed attitude and a habit, and let every lead carry a small joke as well as the strategy.
- **Owlet (Reason):** smug, then exposed. Ends lines with self-praise.
- **Moth-kin (Perception):** breathless and sensory. Starts with "Shh." or "Look."
- **Fox (Imagination):** a show-off director. Bored by the obvious.
- **Frogling (Memory):** an old pond-dweller's anecdotes, deadpan, often about herons.
- **Raven (Language):** a pedant. Quotes one word back, suspiciously.

Rewrites are below. Then re-pick the blind spots so each one is the *tempting* reading at that moment.

### 4. The lecture has moved, not gone

`SIDE-STORIES.md:40` (template beat 6) and every closing question (`:124, 167, 208, 253, 296, 337, 382, 423, 470, 511`) · post-boss stacks at `STORY.md:426, 564 + 590 + 619, 734`

**Problem.** All ten side stories end with the host asking the moral: "Right most of the time. Is that the same as knowing?", "The rule was exact. Was it fair?" Add the four boss questions, and that is fourteen rhetorical TOK questions. Several of them kill the story's best joke. Story 8 ends on Tremendoodle's perfect "'Long'? …That's fair, actually." Then Attenbirdough asks "Did you answer what he said, or what the headline said he said?", which is the moral spelled out, and a tongue-twister for non-native readers.

After each boss, the reaction stack is long. At the Ch2 Hall win: tier scene, jar, Mayor, Algorithm, Quill's last words, suspicion payoff, inner reveal, Sundial question and the Sky Rift. That is 9–10 lines.

**Why it matters.** "Nobody lectures" and "every line earns its place". The baseline's lecture problem is back, just phrased as questions.

**Fix.**
- **Side stories:** end on a character beat or punchline that *implies* the idea. Keep an explicit question in at most three stories (1, 5 and 9 have the best ones). Where a question stays, consider making it a choice with answers rather than a rhetorical line.
- **Bosses:** cap the reaction stack at six lines. Merge the `suspect` payoff into the Sundial's question line.

### 5. Ch3: the twist comes too late, the middle is flat, and the Sundial hasn't been made loved

`STORY.md:642, 659–669, 676, 687–689`, and §3 (`:113–123`)

**Problem.** The complicity twist is excellent: every station you helped Pip with built the case against your friend. But it arrives on the Tribunal Steps, one station before the climax. So the rising action is four Pip stations ("each win is Pip reacting, happily filing") with an anonymous vote counter. That is the baseline's 4/10 chapter in a new coat. Pip also hosts **thirteen** stations across Ch3 and Ch4.

There is a deeper issue too. The rewrite limits the Sundial to arrivals, recaps, one aside and one boss question. That is right for lectures and wrong for love. Nothing in Prologue–Ch2 makes the player *care* about the rock before it is put on trial.

**Why it matters.** The Sundial's possible death is the heaviest beat in the game. It will only land if the player has had small, personal moments with it.

**Fix.**
- **Move the reveal to the midpoint (the Plaza).** The defendant is named, Pip turns pale ("I put it all on the record"), and the second half becomes building the defence while the vote counter climbs between stations. That gives the chapter real rising risk. Keep the Steps for the Speedcheeta negotiation.
- **Give the Sundial one companion beat per chapter.** This means personal moments, not narration. The strongest idea is already in the fiction: a sundial has **never seen night**. In your shadow it sees one for the first time at the Ch1 Campfire (rewrite below). It is warm, absurd and specific, and its death can call back to it.
- **Let the colossus host some Ch4 floors,** so Pip is not the only voice for two chapters.

### 6. The death moments need specific last lines, and one carry-forward is gratuitous

`STORY.md:563, 678, 229, 733`

**Problem.**
- **Granny's death.** She doesn't get a last line. The Sundial delivers a generic eulogy ("She was ninety-three. She was the quickest mind I ever met."). Then "the puzzle goes on": the student must solve stage 3 straight after the loss with no reason given.
- **"SOUP OF THE FUTURE — LIVE".** The imps stream the pot that holds her, and you take the shawl from the stream. That is the one moment in the outline that crosses from cute-dark into mocking the dead, which `UNDERSTUDIES.md` §4 rule 3 rules out.
- **The Sundial's death.** "Tell Granny I—" is the stock movie cut-off.
- **Sequins' death is the model:** "Oh. It's… shiny in there." It is sincere, it is him, and it is wondering rather than afraid.

**Fix.**
- **Granny's last line should give the puzzle its purpose:** "Don't watch the rope, dear. Watch her." Then the rope goes slack and there is steam. The Mayor's "Oh." stays. The Sundial says nothing; save its line for the memorial.
- **Ch3 Plaza:** the shawl turns up on an imp merch stall ("LIMITED EDITION"). It is still dark-comic, but the pot is not streamed.
- **The Sundial should die mid-way through its own catchphrase:** "On cloudy days I gu—". That closes the loop the game opened in its second line.

### 7. Ch1: a lethal tier on the least-loved character, and the peril shape repeats

`STORY.md:429–440, 555`, open question 1 (`:1055`) · `SIDE-STORIES.md:302`

**Problem.** The game's first stakes clock (6 notches, never seen before) can kill Sequins, whom the player has met at two stations. Then in the same week Granny hangs on a rope, and in side story 6 the Mayor hangs from a chain.

**Why it matters.** The first death in the game should fall on someone the player loves, after they have learned to read the clock. Otherwise it feels arbitrary, not tragic. The ladder you designed (friend, then family, then companion) argues for this too. Three danglings also flatten the climaxes.

**Fix.**
- **Take the teacher's alternative:** Ch1 tier 4 is "Influencer Sequins", carried forward and rescued at the Ch2 Square. Deaths start with Granny.
- **Change the Ch1 peril from height to feed-ification.** The clock is Sequins turning into a caricature, one sequin at a time. His warning lines already do this ("I feel… trendier.").
- **Give side story 6 a different peril:** for example, the Mayor stuck in the stocks while the imps' "Fair Trial" crowd throws bad arguments at him.

### 8. `UNDERSTUDIES.md`: the samples rewrite the old lecture text, Kuku and Tally overlap, and the key beats have no lines

`UNDERSTUDIES.md:40, 42, 82–87, 95–99, 108–112, 230–234`

**Problem.**
- Most of the sample tables paraphrase today's rules and lecture lines. "Campfire. Rest is scheduled for now. Health restores. Injured creatures: Mending, in your Bag." The understudies come across as re-skinned tutorials. The doc admits this, but the score has to reflect what is on the page.
- The two understudies most players who lose someone will meet overlap. Kuku is "jittery, punctual, every number said precisely", and Tally is "shy, quiet, precise, little pauses before numbers". Both are nervous, precise counters.
- There are no sample lines for the moments that matter: Kuku popping out at the jar, Tally recognising the Guess-o-Matic, Achilles' character reference at the trial.

What is excellent: Achilles as Zeno's hare ("I'm faster than her. Never caught her, though. Nobody will."), Dawdle ("I hoped it would take… longer."), the memorial's "There were sandwiches" and "Nobody clicks on a candle", and the Algorithm's "SAD CONTENT PERFORMS WELL."

**Fix.**
- **Make Kuku proud and certain, not jittery.** Think brass-band announcer, three hundred years rehearsed. Certainty is the theme of Ch4, and a confident clock is funnier next to the core's collapse.
- **Keep Tally shy.**
- **Replace the old-line samples with one sample per stage-1 understudy at its real beat:** takeover, the key plot beat, and the finale (Kuku sample below).

### 9. Ch2 has too many NPCs, and several have no joke

`STORY.md:512–529, 536, 539`

**Problem.** Fifteen stations and ten speaking villagers for a 30–40 minute lesson. Constable Clobber ("Stranger, eh? From the top."), Miss Whisker ("Wear the cap. Pick a story. Stick to it.") and Mr Thistle ("Things here grow slowly.") are function lines. Granny's hums, Wick and the Mayor carry the comedy alone.

**Fix.** Give each villager one comic angle in their first line, or merge roles. Whisker could gossip while lending the cap ("Everybody's a postman this week. Pick a better story."). Clobber could be the slow, literal bulldog who writes everything down wrong ("Pond… Inspector. Two p's?"). Mr Thistle can be cut from speaking.

### 10. The Prologue climax is overcrowded

`STORY.md:341`

**Problem.** One Nut Stall scene holds: the crack splits, the eye opens, the shadow is stolen, visitors turn, Speedcheeta films, Sequins chases his sequins, the Algorithm notices you, the Algorithm predicts you, Granny's call, the choice, the gifts, the Hum Charm, the Sundial moves into your shadow, and world rule 1. That is about 15 beats, roughly 20 lines.

**Fix.** End the scene on the theft and the prediction ("CONFIDENCE: 94%."), then a beat of silence. Move the gifts, the Hum Charm and the shadow move into a short, quiet follow-up at your door ("I will ride in your shadow. Mind you don't stand in the dark." is a lovely closing line, so give it room).

### 11. Side stories: repeated teasers, a repeated lesson, two weak hooks

`SIDE-STORIES.md:85, 128, 171, 216, 386` (teasers) · `:313, 337` (story 6) · `:433, 476` (stories 9, 10)

**Problem.**
- Five of the ten teasers use "shouting".
- Story 6's twist ("the loud suspect is scared") and Wick's closing question ("How many times was the loud one bad, before?", which is clumsy) repeat the Ch2 boss's lesson in the same chapter.
- Stories 9 and 10 have the weakest openings. "The machine is 90% accurate. It will be better soon." and "Berries for whoever needs them most!" have no danger and no joke in line one.
- The inner-voice hooks share problem 3: mostly instructions.

**Fix.** Give each teaser a different verb and one strange image ("The Witness Tent is tied shut from inside. Someone is dealing cards."). Give story 6 a different reasoning turn: the imp helper is the *honest-looking* one. Open story 9 on a fairgoer smashing their own violin because the machine said "YOU WILL FAIL. 90%". Open story 10 on Granny, ranked last, very slowly entering the circle.

### 12. The climax "picks" are free

`STORY.md:558, 728, 828`

**Problem.** The Ch2 pick is the sentence the Mayor has said all chapter. The Ch3 pick is the boast quoted twice. The Ch4 pick always succeeds. These are fine as a "Take that!" moment, but they are not a climax of reasoning.

**Fix.** Make one wrong option tempting. In Ch3, offer two Algorithm quotes, one of which only proves it *has* the shadow, not that it *stole* it. Keep Ch4 free on purpose, but make the colossus's failed prediction a visible, funny collapse.

**Smaller notes.**
- At the Sorting Room (`STORY.md:812`), "Then the maths can show me how it decides." is avatar-as-teacher. Try "Objective. Lovely. Who does it say no to?"
- Ch3's vote warnings ("GUILTY GUILTY") waste a comedy slot. Let the crowd post bad arguments, which are both the joke and the lesson (below).

## For round 2

- problems 1–3 fixed, with the clue lists cut and the voice tables rewritten;
- problem 5's restructured Ch3;
- problem 2's Ch4 stakes raised;
- the side-story closings reworked;
