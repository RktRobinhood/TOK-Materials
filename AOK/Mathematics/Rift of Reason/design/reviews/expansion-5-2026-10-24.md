## Privacy and planning corrections

Review caught two real information leaks. A privately bottomed known hand card could be inferred from surviving opponent knowledge markers. The engine now forgets all possible pre-choice hand identities from that opponent's `knows`/`sight` records, retaining unrelated known cards. Two different private choices yield identical complete opponent peer DTOs.

Expert also evaluated the actual identity of hypothetical unseen personal draws. `aiPlanning` draws now use unplayable opaque cards with flat hand value. A separate aggregate creature reserve preserves known availability without assigning creatures to unseen positions. The first draft incorrectly treated every opaque card as a creature; a regression now covers all-spell remaining decks and the real `cannot-act` outcome. Public top/bottom recoveries prepare the unknown part before inserting the known card, so those recoveries remain useful in planning. Real matches never acquire planning placeholders. Unknown departures use a conservative bounded reserve estimate; this is a limited planning model, not a claim of optimal memory or a complete information-set AI.

Balanced Exchange answers its disposal choice with the lowest known hand-card value, rather than the recovery heuristic's highest. A fixed 0.2 cycle preference applies only when two deck cards and two draw slots exist. It does not inspect hidden identities, reward merely redrawing the same bottomed card, or tune costs/stats from a win rate. The original 12-seed self-play schedule now actually plays all eight cards.
