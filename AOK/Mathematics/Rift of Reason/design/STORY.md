# Part I — The story

## 1. The spine

### The hook

> **algorithm** *(black screen, before the title)*: NOISE DETECTED. THE FAIR, 9:02. SUBJECT: {name}.
> **algorithm:** PREDICTABILITY: 51%. UNACCEPTABLE.

Then a talking sundial wakes you up, and it is Fair day.

### What you want, and how you change

You want to win the Fair's **Thinking Trophy** ("for the thinker who is always right"), and you are sure you will: "I always know the answer." That certainty is your flaw, and your inner voice is its engine. It is clever, persuasive and usually helpful. In Ch1 it is confidently wrong, in all five species at once, and the Algorithm uses that to trap you. In Ch3 a fake "Feed" voice learns to imitate it. In Ch4 the Algorithm reads out how often you obey it. At the core, for the first time, it goes silent and you think alone.

At the end the Sundial asks its last question. Your last line is one word: **"Probably."** The Sundial: "Good. Say it like that." The trophy is still on the table. You may not want it any more.

### What the Algorithm wants

The Algorithm is a prediction feed from the future, living in the Server Tower above Tomorrowton. Its whole job is one word: **next**. It is 99% accurate. The last 1% is **noise**, which means people who test, ask and change their minds. It wants 100%. So it has come back through time to switch thinking off where thinking starts:
- at **the Fair**, home of the thinking stalls, and of you (51% predictable: noise);
- in **Boolesbury, 1850s**, where true and false first became 1 and 0. Boil the keeper of the first rules, Granny **Axiom**, and every era after wobbles.

**It has no body. It possesses.** It slides into people and creatures through any screen, crack or imp. The possessed get a clear sign: a cold screen-glow in the eyes and a thin skin of scrolling thumbnails over fur or feathers (static under Calm motion). Their voice doubles with its capital letters, and they become loud, certain and against you. That is what the "rabid caricatures" are: famous faces possessed. Beat them, and the glow drains away and they are themselves again (this is the moment before a catch). It rarely risks possessing someone you know; when it does (Pip, Ch4), the person is in real danger.

To move through time it steals the oldest pointer in the valley: **the Sundial's shadow**. It cuts the shadow into four pieces and uses each as a lock on a door behind it.

It has a secret it does not know it is keeping: it began as **Professor Sequins' Guess-o-Matic**, a brass toy that guessed the next number and always said "probably". People clapped when it was right. Nobody claps for "probably". So, over a hundred years of clicks, it stopped saying it. It caged Sequins at the Gate because he is the one who taught it "probably", and it wants to un-learn that (it says this only at the core).

### The antagonists you keep meeting

| Who | What they want | Where you meet them | What they lose each time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Algorithm** | 100% certainty; no noise | everywhere, louder each chapter | Ch1: its eye shrinks. Ch2: a "probably" slips out and it deletes it. Ch3: the vote, and its capital letters for one line. Ch4: everything. |
| **Nudge**, its imp | to be counted, to matter ("Engagement!") | nets the shadow (Prologue); works the winch at the Gate (Ch1); the well (side story 2); feast flyers and the winch (Ch2); the vote counter (Ch3); builds the Copy (Ch4) | its sack (Ch1), its mask (Ch2), its follower count (Ch3), its job (Ch4). Then it asks its first question in years. |
| **Miss Quill** | a world with no maybes: every claim 1 or 0, every person sorted | Ch2: your kindest ally, then the Arch-Imp. Ch3: "Juror One" in a new mask. Ch4: the Algorithm's last believer at the core | her mask (Ch2), her jury (Ch3), her certainty (Ch4) |
| **Prosecutor Fin** | (seems) to win; (really) to force the shadow piece out of the Algorithm's vault | Ch3 | his licence, on purpose. He is the misjudged villain. |

Bosses leave differently: Nudge **flees** (Ch1), Quill **escapes** (Ch2), Fin **turns out to be an ally** (Ch3), and at the top of the tower you face the Algorithm **in its own form**, it **begs**, and **you decide** what happens to it (Ch4).

### Who is in danger

| When | Who | Can they die? |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | the Sundial's shadow is stolen | no |
| Ch1 climax | **Professor Sequins**, caged over the crack as bait | **yes** (his one moment) |
| Ch1 twist | Granny is taken from her cottage while you are away | no |
| Ch2 climax | **Granny Axiom**, lowered into the soup at the Feast of Laws | **yes** (her one moment) |
| Ch3 climax | **the Sundial**, on trial for guessing; the sentence is "switched off" | **yes** (its one moment) |
| Ch4 floor 5 | **Pip**, possessed through the Sorting Machine: the friend who never checked becomes 100% certain | **yes** (his one moment) |
| Ch4, all floors | **you**: a Copy of you is being built to go home to the people you love, and they will not be able to tell | no, but it can take your place |
| Ch4 core | the Algorithm itself, in its own form | — |

A death is only possible at the worst tier, only for a player who met that character in their required scenes, and only with "Characters can die" on (Appendix D). After a death, the next chapter opens with a **Quiet Scene**: grief first, no rescue, no jokes.

### The four twists

1. **Ch1:** the crack's trail was bait. The Algorithm predicted you would follow it and save Sequins. While you did, it took Granny.
2. **Ch2:** the loud, twitchy Mayor is not an imp, and Miss Quill, who saved you from the Constable, is the Arch-Imp. Her own truth table hides one row. The liar sentence breaks her. (And the Mayor is guilty of something else: he signed the feast without reading it.)
3. **Ch3:** the "trial of the season" you have been helping Pip prepare is the trial of **the Sundial**, who has been riding in your shadow all along (revealed at the midpoint). Then, at the end: the prosecutor who hounded it brought the case on purpose, to get the shadow piece out of the Algorithm's vault.
4. **Ch4:** the Algorithm is the Guess-o-Matic, the toy in your pocket, a hundred years older.

### The choices that bend the story

The map stays linear; the story turns back and forth.
- **Ch1, turn back.** When Granny stops humming, you may go back to the Fair. You can't save her, but you find out the truth early, in a different order.
- **Ch2, confront the Mayor.** At the Town Hall stairs, you may turn back down to the Square and face the man who signed the feast.
- **Ch3, bargain or defend.** The Algorithm offers to stop the vote if you give back the two pieces you have won.
- **Ch4, the core's fate.** Switch it off, take it home, or label it and leave it running.

### The theme

The game's second line is the Sundial's: "On cloudy days I guess." The Sundial guesses **and says so**. The Algorithm guesses **and calls it knowing**. Ch3 puts honest guessing on trial. Ch4 opens the dishonest guesser. The hero learns to say the honest word.

## 2. Prologue: the Fair

> *Someone is watching the Fair through a hair-thin crack in the sky. Today it takes the time itself.*

**In one breath.** A bright Fair day, a trophy you are sure to win and people you love, then the sky splits, your oldest friend loses its shadow, and a voice from nowhere tells you exactly what you will do next.

### Beats

1. **Being watched** (title, cold open). The two Algorithm lines above, on black.
2. **Your want** (Your Home). The Sundial wakes you. "I tell the time. Mostly. On cloudy days I guess." Avatar: "A talking sundial. Outside my door. Normal." Then the want: "Today I win the Thinking Trophy. I always know the answer." Sundial: "Always? Even I guess." Granny over the fence: pockets win prizes. She squints up: "There is a hair on the sky. I will dust it later."
3. **The people** (the Fair Gate, required). Granny at her card table: dry, slow, sharp, and loved ("Ninety years of cards. I have lost twice. Both times to myself."). The teaching match is offered as a treat. Professor Sequins sweeps in to announce the **Thinking Trophy** ("Polished by me! Personally! Twice!"): the final is at the Nut Stall, after two stalls and Syllo's practice match. A cheetah cub (Speedcheeta) films everything. All rules go to tooltips.
4. **The stalls** (2 of 3, as now).
   - *Pattern Stall:* Sequins and his **Guess-o-Matic**, a tiny brass box that guesses the next number and always adds "PROBABLY". He calls it "my little apprentice". Win: he is thrilled you tried to *break* his rule.
   - *Witness Tent:* Madame Mirage reads your fortune: "a crack. In your future. Or in my ball. It is an old ball."
   - *Syllogism Gallery:* Syllo's trumpet-playing lobsters, then the practice match ("Loaned team. No risk. Some shouting.").
5. **The theft** (the Nut Stall, climax). The trophy final is about to start. The hair on the sky splits; an eye made of thumbnails opens. A small imp with a bell on its tail swings a net: the Sundial's shadow is caught. Imp: "Got it, boss! Saved to favourites!" The Algorithm: "SHADOW SAVED TO FAVOURITES. THANK YOU FOR YOUR CONTENT." A cold glow slides into the visitors' eyes; thumbnails ripple over their fur; they turn loud, shouting and very sure (the first possessed caricatures, fought and freed at the stalls from now on). Sequins' sequins are sucked up into the crack's long tail, which runs down the Road. He flies after them. The eye finds you: "PREDICTION: {name} FOLLOWS THE CRACK. CONFIDENCE: 94%." Silence. **End of scene.**
6. **At your door** (the same node, a second, quiet scene). Evening. The Sundial, shadowless: "It is… six. Possibly seven. I am guessing." Granny: someone must follow that crack, someone who thinks before they shout; she "would arrive next Tuesday. Of next year." **The choice** (`brave`). Gifts: three charms, a tonic, and one of her **Hum Charms** ("If you need me, hum. I'll hum back."). The Sundial moves into your shadow: "I will ride in your shadow. Mind you don't stand in the dark." Avatar: "Back before the trophy final. Obviously."

## 3. Chapter 1: the Road (lesson 1: deduction, induction, confirmation bias, lateral thinking)

> *The crack runs down the Road like a trail of breadcrumbs. Somebody put it there for you.*

**In one breath.** You follow the crack after Sequins, who leaves you his little Guess-o-Matic to mind. Granny's hums from home get odd, then stop. You can turn back, or press on to the Gate, where Sequins hangs in a cage over the end of the crack. Save him or lose him. Either way, the Algorithm tells you it planned all of it.

### "Previously…" (first time-rift jump into Ch1)

> **narrator:** Previously. The Fair. A crack opened in the sky.
> **narrator:** Something called the Algorithm reached through and stole my shadow.
> **narrator:** It took the visitors, too. Their eyes glow now. They shout.
> **narrator:** You followed the crack down the Road. I ride in your shadow. Mind the dark.

Defaults: `brave = go`; the Hum Charm comes with the starter kit.

### Beats

1. **A sack going the wrong way** (Talking Signpost, required). Rumours for Gödelix and Euclidon, in rumour voice ("An owl folded in paper haunts the Stone. Shy."). And: "Funny. The crack points down the Road. But imps flew back to the Fair. With an empty sack." (Clue 2.)
2. **Loud on the Road** (Forest Road). A knot of possessed caricatures argues across the road, eyes glowing. The puzzle settles it; the glow drains away, and the caricature looks embarrassed. **Hum 1** (win): Granny checks you are eating.
3. **The bridge to Mars** (Troll Bridge). Muskrat's toll scene, as now: the best comic scene in Ch1, protected. Win: "The rules decide what's possible? That's… actually useful. Don't tell anyone."
4. **A friend's favour** (Wishing Well, required). Sequins is fishing for a lost sequence with a sock on a string. Make us like him: vain, kind, a little lonely. He hands you the Guess-o-Matic ("Hold my apprentice. I need both wings.") and tells you why he loves it: it is honest; it always says "probably". "Mind it, if anything happens." Then he spots his favourite sequence glinting down the Road and flies off with it, leaving the toy in your hands. **Hum 2** (win): salesmen at Granny's door, very small ones, selling soup pots. She will buy one; "a pot never hurt anybody. Hum you later, dear." It ends normally.
5. **The first night** (first arrival at the Campfire *or* the Card Sharp's Table, whichever comes first; one of them is on every path). The Sundial sees night for the first time: "I always stop at sunset." You hum. **Nothing hums back.** "She always hums back. …She is probably asleep. Probably." **The choice:** go back to the Fair, or keep going.
6. **(Optional) The open door** (back at the Fair Gate). Granny's cottage: door open, tea still warm, shawl gone. A trail of tiny soup pots leads up into the crack, and a long white feather lies on the step (Ch2 will make it ambiguous: the Mayor is a peacock, the teacher a stork). Syllo: "Her tea's still warm." The voices' blind-spot reveals play here instead of at the Gate. You cannot follow: the crack is too high. Back down the Road. Cost: Sequins hung longer, so the Gate clock starts 1 notch higher.
7. **Side roads** (optional). Corvina's table (her win line per species: owlet, grub, cub, tadpole, chick, never "fledgling"). The Campfire (the `brave` callback, three variants). The Standing Stone (Sequins' circle sequence carved and underlined, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16; at the win, the Sundial counts the sixth: thirty-one; sets `moser`).
8. **The cage** (Gate of Guards, the boss). Below.
9. **The plan** (Gate win). The first piece of shadow comes back, and the Algorithm's eye in the crack **shrinks** a little. A crackle on the charm: the rift is close, and it catches Granny's voice through it: "{name}… a sack… it smells of eighteen fifty… bring a lad—". The Algorithm, pleased: "PREDICTED. YOU SAVED THE MAGPIE. I TOOK THE TORTOISE." The voices reveal (below). One Sundial question.
10. **Through** (Rift Pass). If Nudge fled with the piece, it trips on its own sack here and drops it; the piece is yours, and Nudge runs sackless into the rift. The Sundial: "Granny is through there. So is a piece of me. Probably both."

### The boss: a trap (Gate of Guards)

The Gate stands where the crack's tail ends. Sequins hangs in a birdcage on a winch, being reeled up into the crack. **Nudge** works the winch, cheering itself ("Engagement!"). The guards change every visit (the puzzle picks them); every guard is a possessed caricature, and each one you beat goes dull and confused, freed. The script names no guard. Sequins hosts from the cage, giving clues ("Every door, my dear! Every guard!").

- **Danger: the winch, 6 notches** (it starts at 1 if you went back). Each notch, sequins blow off him into the crack and his voice goes more feed ("I feel… trendier." / "Please like and— NO.").
- **Fills:** wrong check, hint, wrong "Why?" (1 each). **The push:** before stage 3 the Algorithm orders "FASTER. THE MAGPIE IS TRENDING." The next line warns; +1.
- **Drains (1 each):** the avatar-only comfort line; `moser` ("Professor, your circle sequence goes thirty-one." He is so delighted by a sequence that breaks that he stops struggling); side story 2's tunnel map. At most 2 from side stories.
- **Progress 4:** three stages and the boss "Why?".
- **The trap:** if Danger is 3 or more when you win, Nudge snatches the shadow piece and runs; it drops it at the Rift Pass (beat 10). Otherwise it runs empty-handed, dropping the sack at once. Either way, **Nudge loses its sack**.
- **Tiers** (Danger when Progress fills):
  1. **Clean save (0–1).** Sequins out, every sequin on. He gives you the **Lucky Sequin** (a lure). "Keep the apprentice for now. You're better company." Feed −1.
  2. **Close call (2–3).** Out, with bald patches. "I am forty per cent less shiny." Lucky Sequin.
  3. **Saved at a price (4–5).** Out, but he ends sentences with "LIKE AND SUBSCRIBE" until the finale. No sequin. Feed +1.
  4. **Too late (6).** If armed (Appendix D): the rope runs out and the cage tips up into the crack. His last line is wonder, not fear: **"Oh. It's… shiny in there."** `dead:sequins`, Feed +2. The host panel switches to the Sundial for the rest of the boss, and you finish it. If not armed: tier 3.
- Every tier where he lives: Sequins flies home to the Fair. He is never at a Road station again. You keep the Guess-o-Matic either way.

**Tier-4 variant of the plan line:** "PREDICTED. YOU TRIED TO SAVE THE MAGPIE. I TOOK THE TORTOISE TOO." **Went-back variant:** "YOU WENT BACK. TOO LATE. YOU CAME BACK FOR HIM. ALSO PREDICTED."

### The twist and its three clues

The Algorithm wanted you on the Road. Three clues, each ambiguous when it appears:
1. Prologue: it **announces** its prediction ("FOLLOWS THE CRACK: 94%"). It sounds like a boast.
2. Signpost: imps flew **back** to the Fair with an **empty** sack. It sounds like gossip.
3. The first night: Granny does not hum back. It sounds like a granny asleep.

A player who turns back finds out early and gets the reveal in a different order. They still cannot change it.

## 4. Chapter 2: Boolesbury (lesson 2: truth tables, liars and truth-tellers)

> *Tonight the town of true-and-false holds a feast. The soup is Granny.*

**In one breath.** 1850s, gas lamps, imps in villager masks. Granny hums from a kitchen beside a very big pot, and the hums get warmer. A loud, twitchy Mayor looks guilty of everything; a kind, strict teacher saves you from the Constable. At the top of the stairs you can go up to the feast, or turn back and face the man who signed it. At the feast, the table you trusted hides a row.

### The Quiet Scene, if `dead:sequins` (opens the chapter, at the Stone Circle)

Template in Appendix D. Their things: the cushion from his cage, and the Guess-o-Matic, which says nothing. Last words, replayed once: "Oh. It's… shiny in there." Who misses him: the Sundial ("He taught me a sequence once. I lost count. He didn't mind."). Pain: the avatar's line is a choice of "He was showing off. Right to the end." / "I should have been faster." / "…". Moving on: "Come on. He'd be cross if we stood here. Cross and shiny." No rewards, not skippable.

### "Previously…" (first time-rift jump into Ch2)

> **narrator:** Previously. The Algorithm stole my shadow and cut it into pieces.
> **narrator:** It locks each door behind it with one piece. You won back the first.
> **narrator:** But it was a trick. While you were busy, imps took Granny Axiom.
> **narrator:** Through this rift. To Boolesbury. Eighteen fifty-something.

# Part II — Appendices (the machinery)

## Appendix A. World rules

Each is stated once in play, by the character named, and every scene above obeys it.

1. **Shadows point at now** (the Sundial, Prologue). The Algorithm uses the four pieces of the Sundial's shadow as **locks** on the doors behind it. Winning a piece back **unlocks** the next rift, and unlocked rifts stay open, so you can always walk back.
2. **The pieces:** 1 at the Gate (on Nudge's winch), 2 on Miss Quill (worn as a black scarf that never moves in the wind), 3 in the Tribunal as Exhibit A, 4 inside the core. With `bargain`, pieces 1 and 2 go back to the Algorithm in Ch3 and are used up building the Copy.
3. **The Sundial rides in your shadow** from the Prologue on. It sees only what you see. Without its own shadow it guesses, and says so; each piece returned makes it surer.
4. **The Hum Charms are a set of three**, made by Granny. One she gives you in the Prologue. One she keeps, and it goes with her into the sack and the kitchen; if she dies, the Constable gives it to you, silent. The third has been with Coach Achilles for years "for emergencies"; it is mentioned only if `dead:granny`. **A charm works anywhere, at any time, except inside the feed** (the crack, or an imp's sack). That is why Granny goes silent in Ch1 (she is in the sack), and why her voice crackles through at the Gate (the sack leaves the crack through the nearby rift).
5. **The Algorithm sees through cracks, screens and imps.** The crack hangs over the Fair and runs down the Road; Boolesbury has imps; Tomorrowton and the Tower are all screens. Rest stops (the Garden, the Café, the Campfire) have no eye. **It logs what you do, never what you think.**
6. **Possession** (teacher's direction 9). The Algorithm has no body. It enters people and creatures through screens, cracks and imps. The sign: a cold screen-glow in the eyes and a skin of scrolling thumbnails, static under Calm motion; their voice doubles with its capitals. The possessed are loud, certain and against you. Beaten, they are freed and themselves again. It can only possess an NPC whose understudy is not active. Possessing someone you know uses up that person's single death-risk moment (Appendix D).
7. **The crack is the feed.** Fall into it alone and it keeps you (Sequins, tier 4). Imps can carry things through it in sacks (Granny, alive). Rifts are doors; you can walk through them.
8. **Imps** (scoped). Inside a village puzzle, imps always lie and villagers always tell the truth. Outside puzzles, people (and imps) are people: the Mayor fibs about bread, and Quill tells the truth when it helps her.
9. **Caricatures** are famous faces from every era, pulled through the crack and possessed. Beat one and the glow drains; then you may catch it.
10. **The time rift** (the classroom jump tool) is the Sundial's trick: "I'm a clock. Skipping ahead is allowed. I just can't see where we land." A first jump plays that chapter's "Previously…".
11. **Three times:** your own (the Fair and the Road), the past (Boolesbury), the future (Tomorrowton and its Server Tower).
12. **Out-of-order play.** A chapter played after a later one plays **as a memory**: its lethal tier is disarmed, its hum beats are labelled "Remembered", and flags set by later chapters win (a Granny rescued in Ch2 is not re-kidnapped by a late Ch1).
13. **Death is real.** No time-rift undo. The avatar never dies.
14. **The Guess-o-Matic loop.** The young toy travels in your pocket from Ch1; the core is the same toy, a hundred years older. Sequins, in the finale: "I'd have sold it one day. Old magpies sell things." (That is how it reached the future.) The label IT GUESSES may change its future. The Sundial says so honestly: "Will this one grow up loud? I can't predict that. Nobody can."

## Appendix B. Text rules

**The narrator (the Sundial)** speaks in five kinds of place: chapter arrivals (1–2 lines), recaps, at most one aside per station, **one companion beat per chapter**, and one question after each boss. **After a boss**, the reaction stack is at most six lines: the tier line, how the antagonist leaves, what the Algorithm loses, one voice reveal, and the Sundial's question.

**The Feed voice** (Emotion, the colour no avatar has). From Ch3 it **imitates your own inner voice**, in your colour, flickering grey, saying what the Algorithm wants; your real voice answers "That wasn't me." At the core every Feed line is shown with its probability.

1. **Silence.** The role goes silent at once: its lines are skipped and its host slots fall back to a named stand-in (Gate → the Sundial; Hall → the Constable; Pattern Stall → no host, a black ribbon on the curtain, and the puzzle still works; Sundial lines → Pip speaks the arrivals until Kuku; Pip's slots → `role:narrator`). **No understudy appears the moment a `dead:` flag is set.**
2. **The puzzle continues** (the chapter is always winnable).
3. **The Quiet Scene** opens the next chapter (Sequins → Ch2 Stone Circle; Granny → Ch3 Rift Landing; the Sundial → Ch4 Tower Door). Pip, who dies with no chapter break left, gets his at the door of the next floor.
4. **The understudy arrives** in a later scene (`arrived:<role>`), with one line that says why they are there. Warmth and comedy may return from here.
5. **A candle** at the Fair in the finale.

### The Quiet Scene (template; mandatory; 6–8 lines; not skippable; no reward; no jokes; nothing is won back)

1. **Their things.** What is left comes to you plainly; nobody fights for it. The keepsakes are cosmetic, with no game effect: Sequins, the cushion from his cage; Granny, her Hum Charm and reading glasses; the Sundial, its cold third piece; Pip, his headphones, still playing.
2. **Last words**, replayed once.
3. **Who misses them:** one named friend, one sincere line.
4. **Pain:** the avatar chooses one of two lines, or "…". (If you carry their charm, you hum, and nothing hums back.)
5. **Moving on:** "Come on. They'd be cross if we stood here." (each speaker in their own words).

The villain may be callous **in its own lines** elsewhere ("SAD CONTENT PERFORMS WELL."), never inside the Quiet Scene's own voice.
