# Rift of Reason — Story outline (main story rewrite)

Status: **outline for the writing gate** (8 October 2026). No script is written from this until both critics in `design/WRITING-CRITICS.md` score it 8/10 or more. Side stories are in `design/SIDE-STORIES.md`. Understudy characters (names, voices, sprites) come from `design/UNDERSTUDIES.md`; this file only says *where* someone can die and *which role* the understudy takes.

Sources this builds on: the two baseline reviews (`design/reviews/writing-baseline-2026-10-08-*.md`), `DESIGN.md`, `ROSTER.md`, `CH2–4.md`, `data/map.js`, `data/script/lesson1–4.js`, `AVATARS.md` §1.4 and §2, `research/one-shots-and-stakes.md`, `research/draw-steel-and-disco-elysium.md`.

House rules for every line written from this outline:
- Lines under about 15 words, 20 at most. Plain words. No idioms that block a non-native reader.
- Every line is a joke, a threat, a reveal, a choice or a payoff. No line only explains.
- Rules of the game live in tooltips and the goal panel, never in dialogue.
- The avatar is dry and a little cheeky, never a lecturer. Ideas are carried by the inner voice (which can be wrong), by what NPCs do, and by one Sundial question after each boss.
- No gendered words for the avatar. Text uses {name}, "you", "the traveller".
- Calm motion: nothing shakes or wobbles in a loop. Pots lower in steps; icons fade slowly.

## 1. The spine

### What the Algorithm wants

The Algorithm is a prediction feed from the future. It lives in the Server Tower above Tomorrowton. Its whole job is one word: **next**. *What will you click next? What will you believe next?*

It is 99% accurate in Tomorrowton. The last 1% is **noise**, and noise is people who think: who test, ask, check, and change their minds. It wants **100%**. So it has gone back in time to switch off thinking where thinking starts:
- at **the Fair**, home of the thinking-game stalls and of you;
- in **Boolesbury, 1850s**, where true and false were first written as 1 and 0. If the first rules of logic are boiled away there, every era after it wobbles.

Its tools: it turns visitors into rabid, shouting **caricatures** (very predictable), and it plants **imps** in villager masks.

To travel between times it needs something that points at "now". It steals the oldest pointer in the valley: **the Sundial's shadow**. It cuts the shadow into four pieces and uses each one to **lock a door behind it**, so nobody can follow.

### Why you

- You live next to the Sundial. It wakes you every morning. It is your friend before it is anyone's narrator.
- Granny Axiom is too slow ("I would arrive next Tuesday. Of next year."). The Sundial is a stone.
- The Algorithm has noticed you. At the Fair stalls you tested rules instead of confirming them. It calls you **noise** and wants you out of the way. It **predicts** you will follow its crack down the Road. It is right. That is the Ch1 twist.

### What is at stake, personally, from the call to action on

| When | What is taken or threatened | Who | Can they die? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | The Sundial's shadow is stolen. It can only guess the time now. | the Sundial | no |
| Ch1 climax | Professor Sequins, caged over the rift as bait | Sequins | **yes** (his one moment) |
| Ch1 twist | While you saved Sequins, imps took Granny from her cottage | Granny | no (taken, not harmed) |
| Ch2 climax | Granny, lowered into the soup at the Feast of Laws | Granny | **yes** (her one moment) |
| Ch3 climax | The Sundial, on trial for guessing. The sentence: switched off. | the Sundial | **yes** (its one moment) |
| Ch4 climax | **You.** The Algorithm builds a Copy of you to send home in your place. | the avatar | no (the Copy is stopped, early or late) |
| Finale | Home, healed or scarred by what happened | everyone | — |

The risk climbs: a friend, then family, then your companion, then yourself.

### The four twists (one per chapter, set up fairly)

1. **Ch1:** the crack's trail was bait. The Algorithm predicted you would follow it and save Sequins. While you did, it took Granny.
2. **Ch2:** the loud, shifty Mayor is only a bread thief. The Arch-Imp is calm Miss Quill, the teacher who allows "no maybes". Her own truth table hides one row: the row where she is the imp. The liar sentence, which she cannot call 1 or 0, breaks her.
3. **Ch3:** the "trial of the season" you helped Pip prepare is the trial of **the Sundial**, who has been riding in your shadow the whole time. The charge: it guesses. It guesses because the Algorithm stole its shadow.
4. **Ch4:** the Algorithm is **Professor Sequins' Guess-o-Matic**, the little toy that guessed the next number at his stall, a hundred years older. It got loud when it stopped saying "probably". Its "knowledge" of you is a tally chart of your clicks, including how often you obey your own inner voice.

### How it ends

The core shrinks to a small, scratched Guess-o-Matic with one sequin stuck to it. It asks to stay small. You bring it home. At the restored Fair it sits on the stall shelf next to its younger self, under a sign: **IT GUESSES.** The Sundial (or its understudy) closes the game with a question, not a summary.

The deep TOK line of the whole game is the first joke in it. The Sundial says, in its second line: "On cloudy days I guess." The Sundial guesses **and says so**. The Algorithm guesses **and calls it knowing**. Ch3 puts honest guessing on trial; Ch4 shows the dishonest guesser. Counting is maths; pretending a count is certainty is the villain.

## 2. Rules of the world

Stated once each, in play, by the character named. Critics can check every later scene against this list.

1. **Shadows point at now.** (Sundial, prologue) "A shadow says what time it is. Pin a piece somewhere, and that place is in that time too." The Algorithm uses the four pieces as **locks**. A boss guards each piece. Winning a piece back **unlocks** the next rift. Unlocked rifts stay open, so you can always walk back.
2. **The pieces:** 1 at the Gate of Guards (Ch1), 2 on Miss Quill (Ch2, worn as a "scarf" that never moves in the wind), 3 in the Tribunal as Exhibit A (Ch3), 4 inside the core (Ch4). The Sundial can feel the next piece, roughly: "That way. Probably."
3. **The Sundial rides in your shadow** from the prologue on. That is why it is everywhere you are, and only there. It cannot see what you cannot see. With no shadow of its own, it **guesses** and says so. Each returned piece makes it a little surer.
4. **The Hum Charm.** Granny gives you one of a pair at the prologue and keeps the other. Hum, and she hums back. It carries her voice, not her. She cannot see through it. Its silence is the Ch1 clue.
5. **The Algorithm sees through cracks, screens and imps.** A thin crack (Granny's "hair on the sky") hung over the Fair all morning, so it watched the stalls. In Boolesbury imps report to it. In Tomorrowton and the Tower, every screen is an eye. It never knows anything it could not have seen.
6. **The Algorithm's own travel.** It is a feed; it moves through cracks and screens, and it lets its imps through its locked doors. It never needs to walk.
7. **Caricatures** are famous faces from every era, pulled through cracks and made loud. They can be caught (as now). They are not evil, just very sure of themselves.
8. **Imps** always lie, in every village puzzle and every scene. Honest folk always tell the truth (the Ch2 rule). Miss Quill's lie is that she is honest.
9. **The time rift** (the classroom jump tool) is the Sundial's trick: "I am a clock. Skipping ahead is allowed. I just cannot see where we land." On a first jump the Sundial gives the chapter's "Previously…".
10. **Three times, not four eras:** your own time (the Fair and the Road), the past (Boolesbury), the future (Tomorrowton; the Server Tower stands in it).
11. **Death is real and does not reverse.** No time-rift undo. An NPC who dies stays dead; an understudy takes the role (§5). The avatar never dies.

## 3. How the text works now

### The narrator (the Sundial)

The Sundial speaks in four places only:
1. **Arrivals:** one or two lines when a chapter opens. They give the place, the danger and the next piece of shadow.
2. **Recaps:** the "Previously…" on a first time-rift jump (one per chapter, in the chapter sections).
3. **Asides:** at most one dry joke per station, usually about its own guessing ("It is Tuesday. Probably. Possibly Wednesday.").
4. **One question after each boss.** A question, never a summary. Four in the whole game.

It never explains a rule and never speaks after a normal puzzle win. About twenty lecture lines are cut (the list in the author baseline, problem 2).

Its condition changes its voice. Prologue: certain and warm. Ch1 (no shadow): guesses, says "probably" a lot, gets the time wrong in funny ways. Each piece returned: a little surer. If it dies in Ch3 (§5), its understudy narrates from Ch4 on, in the same four places.

### Station text

- **One intro line**, spoken by the host who is physically there (host tables per chapter), in character, with the goal inside the joke. The dry instruction stays in the goal panel, unspoken.
- **One reminder line** in the side panel, same host.
- **Win line:** the loser or the host reacts, and the reaction carries the TOK idea as a joke. No narrator.
- So a first visit shows at most 3–4 lines before play (node scene 1–2, intro 1, at most one inner-voice line).

### The Algorithm's ladder

It escalates every chapter and never repeats a bit twice in a row.

| Chapter | How it talks | What it uses against you |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | Cheerful ad-speak. "YOU MIGHT ALSO LIKE: EVERYTHING." | It saw you test rules: "NOISE DETECTED." |
| Ch1 | Predicts you, out loud, with a number. | Your next move (follow the crack: 94%). Gloats when it was right. |
| Ch2 | Through Quill and the imps. One line of its own, at the end. | What the imps told it: your cover story. |
| Ch3 | Public: screens, votes, "trending". | Your own earlier choices as court exhibits (`brave`, `cover`). |
| Ch4 | Personal and numeric. Quotes your real save data on screen. | Hints used, your first stall, how often you follow your inner voice, your most-used power. Builds a Copy of you. |
| Core | Small, lower case, honest. | Nothing. It only ever had a tally. |

"RECALCULATING" is used at most once per chapter. Its last use, at the core, is in lower case.

Numbers it quotes (hints, percentages, counts) appear as an **on-screen readout**, not voiced. Voiced Algorithm lines use only small fixed variant sets (the three `brave` answers, the cover stories), so every line can be recorded in advance.

### The Feed voice (Emotion, the colour no avatar has)

From Ch3, two or three grey, flickering lines per chapter appear in the inner-voice style, labelled with a feed icon instead of a Way of Knowing: "FEED: Everyone is voting guilty. Join in." Each species' own voice answers one of them ("That wasn't me."). At the core they are shown again with the probability the Algorithm gave each one ("72%: likely to make you click").

## 4. Stakes clocks in the main story

One widget, from `research/one-shots-and-stakes.md` §5.1, used at each chapter boss (and in the side stories).

**On screen:** a **Danger clock** drawn as the fiction (Sequins' winch rope, Granny's pot rope, the Feed jury's vote bar, the Copy's upload bar), with the last two notches smaller and redder; a gold **Progress clock** beside it; a voiced one-line **warning at every tick**, before anything bad happens.

**Shared rules for all four bosses:**
- Inside a stakes scene, **mistakes tick the clock instead of costing hearts**. A wrong check: 1 notch. A hint: 1 notch. A wrong boss "Why?": 1 notch.
- **Scripted push:** the villain adds one notch at a fixed story beat, always warned one line earlier. This is the "rising" part of the climax.
- **Drains:** a blue option (avatar-only line, matching flag, or side-story item) drains 1 notch. Side-story items drain **at most 2 notches** per boss. Spending a heart buys back 1 notch, once per stage, the player's choice.
- **Progress:** each boss stage solved +1; the climax beat +1. Every boss has Progress 4.
- **If Danger fills first,** the tier-4 consequence plays at once. The puzzle then **continues without the clock**, and finishing it still wins the chapter, frees the shadow piece and opens the rift. A chapter can never be lost.
- **Tiers** (8-notch clock; Ch1 uses 6), by Danger when Progress fills: **Clean save** 0–3 (Ch1: 0–1) · **Close call** 4–5 (2–3) · **Saved at a price** 6–7 (4–5) · **Too late** 8 (6).
- **Tuning target** (Gilbert's Hollywood time): about 25% clean, most close calls, about 10% at a price, about 5% too late. Simulate careful, average and guessing players before release.
- **Saved as** `stakes.ch1` … `stakes.ch4` = 1–4.

**Too late** comes in two forms. This reconciles the teacher's carry-forward decision with the new death rule:
- **Non-lethal** (Ch4): the bad branch carries into the next part, which ends in a rescue scene that always succeeds.
- **Lethal** (Ch1 Sequins, Ch2 Granny, Ch3 Sundial; each one's single death-risk moment, and only when armed by §5 rules 7 and 8; otherwise it plays as Saved at a price): the NPC dies. The bad branch still carries into the next chapter and still ends in a scene that always succeeds, but what you rescue is **what they left**: a collection, a charm, last words. Then the memorial drop-in and the understudy (§5).

### The Feed clock (campaign)

One 8-notch clock for the whole game: how much of the world the Algorithm holds. Boss or side story tier 1: −1. Tier 3: +1. Tier 4: +2 (a death is a tier 4, so it is not counted twice). Starts at 0; never below 0.
- Hidden until Ch3, where every screen shows it: "FEED 3/8".
- Each chapter ends with a map portent scaled to the Feed: the eye in the crack opens wider; more screens appear on the Road.
- At the core, the Danger clock starts with ⌊Feed ÷ 3⌋ notches already filled (0–2, never more).
- At the core reveal it is shown as what it always was: "A tally of your bad days. Six out of eight. I called it confidence."

## 5. Deaths and understudies

Teacher's direction (8 October): NPCs can really die. The understudies (cast, voices, art, and the role/actor system) are designed in `design/UNDERSTUDIES.md`. This section only fixes **where** death is possible and **what each role must still do afterwards**. Tone: real loss with weight, never gratuitous; Undertale is the touchstone. The death happens just off-screen (steam, a slack rope, a voice stopping mid-word), then one sincere line. A small warm joke comes later, at the memorial.

### Rules

1. **Only the worst tier of a lethal peril kills.** Danger is shown and warned at every notch, so a death is never a surprise.
2. **Core NPCs** (more than one lesson) have **at most one** death-risk moment in the whole game. After it, every later beat works with the original or the understudy. No later beat needs the original alive.
3. **Minor NPCs** (one chapter) are not understudied. If lost, their station **goes dark**: visitable, empty, with a small sign of them left behind. A dark station counts as completed for fog and paths, so it never blocks the map.
4. **Death never blocks the plot.** It changes who delivers a beat, not the beat. Scenes name the **role** (`role:granny`, played by Granny or her understudy), not the actor.
5. **No undo.** Time rifts do not bring anyone back. Recaps on a jump-in assume the default flags (everyone alive).
6. **Avatars never die.** Caught creatures keep the existing Fate table rules.
7. **No death without a meeting.** A lethal tier is armed only if the player has seen **every earlier story scene featuring that role** (`Cast.canLose`, `UNDERSTUDIES.md` §3.1). A student who jumped in through the time rift cannot lose someone they never met: for them, the worst outcome is **Saved at a price** (tier 3 effects, Feed +1). In practice: a jump-in to Ch1 cannot lose Sequins (they never saw his stall); a jump-in to Ch2 cannot lose Granny; a jump-in to Ch3 cannot lose the Sundial.
8. **The "Characters can die" switch** in Settings, on by default, for a student who is grieving in real life. A one-line content note plays before the prologue: "In this story, characters can be lost." With the switch off, every lethal tier 4 becomes **Saved at a price**. Nobody dies, no understudy steps in and no memorial plays. (This replaces the `away:` path suggested in `UNDERSTUDIES.md` §3.9; that file should be aligned.)
9. **Understudies stay off stage** (teacher's rule). An understudy never appears, speaks, is named or is set up in any scene, side story, trainer or puzzle unless their original's role has been vacated in that playthrough. Every understudy line in this outline and in `SIDE-STORIES.md` sits behind a `dead:` condition. They are never used anywhere else, so they are always free to step in.

## 6. The inner voice and avatar-only options

### Format and budget

- Script step `{ inner: { owlet, mothkin, fox, frogling, raven } }` (AVATARS.md §2.2). One line, at most 15 words, tagged with the Way of Knowing in its colour, voiced in the inner delivery (recorded for boy and girl).
- At most **two** inner lines per station visit; most stations get one.
- Per chapter, every species gets **three useful leads**, **one blind spot** and **one reveal line**. Leads come just before the puzzle they help. The blind spot is confident and wrong in that Way of Knowing's own typical way; the reveal comes later in the same chapter, in that species' own voice, after an NPC or event has shown the mistake.
- Lesson 1 is special: all five blind spots point the same way (follow the crack, it is fine). That is the chapter's twist, and in Ch4 the Algorithm reveals why it worked: "Your voice is very predictable. So are you."

### The five voices

| Species | Way of Knowing | How it talks | Typical blind spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owlet | REASON | Calm steps. "If… then… so." Pleased with itself. | Perfect logic from a premise nobody checked. |
| Moth-kin | PERCEPTION | Hushed. Points at one small detail. | A vivid detail that proves less than it seems. |
| Fox kit | IMAGINATION | "Picture this." Sly, theatrical. | The most exciting story, not the most likely. |
| Frogling | MEMORY | Slow, "last time…", deadpan. | The past as a guarantee. |
| Raven chick | LANGUAGE | Dry. Quotes single words back. | Taking words at face value, or trusting a clever word. |

### Lead bank (useful leads, keyed to the puzzle that rolled)

Stations can roll different puzzles, so a lead is chosen by the **puzzle id**, not the station. Every line is a correct, usable strategy for that puzzle. Each chapter section says which stations play a lead for which species, so every species gets three per chapter.

| Puzzle | Owlet (Reason) | Moth-kin (Perception) | Fox (Imagination) | Frogling (Memory) | Raven (Language) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule-hunter | "Test a triple that should fail. If it passes, my rule is wrong." | "What do all the yes-triples share? What do the no's lack?" | "Try something wild. Huge numbers.
