Status: **outline, round 4 of the writing gate** (8 October 2026). Three critics (logic, author, editor) must each score 8/10 before any script is written (`design/WRITING-CRITICS.md`). The teacher's direction is binding (`design/TEACHER-STORY-NOTES.md`). Side stories: `design/SIDE-STORIES.md`. Understudy cast: `design/UNDERSTUDIES.md`, which follows **Appendix D** of this file wherever they differ.

**How to read this file.** Part I is the story: the spine, then the chapters in play order. Part II (the appendices) holds the machinery: world rules, text rules, stakes clocks, deaths and understudies, inner voices, flags, continuity, implementation, and the change lists for each round.

House rules for every line written from this outline: lines under about 15 words (20 at most); every line is a joke, a threat, a reveal, a choice or a payoff; rules live in tooltips, never in dialogue; no gendered words for the avatar ({name}, "you"); no idioms that block a non-native reader; calm motion; nobody lectures; the story's own voice never mocks a death.

# Part I — The story

## 1. The spine

### The hook

> **algorithm** *(black screen, before the title)*: NOISE DETECTED. THE FAIR, 9:02. SUBJECT: {name}.
> **algorithm:** PREDICTABILITY: 51%. UNACCEPTABLE.

Then a talking sundial wakes you up, and it is Fair day.

### What you want, and how you change

You want the Fair's **Thinking Trophy** ("for the thinker who is always right"), and at the Nut Stall, as the final opens right under a hair-thin crack in the sky, you tell Professor Sequins you will win it: **"I always know the answer."** Something up there hears you.

That certainty is your flaw. The middle of the game makes it cost you, on stage, once a chapter, and the Algorithm quotes your boast back each time:

| Chapter | You are sure… | …and it costs you | The Algorithm quotes you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ch1 | "She's asleep. Obviously." (Granny, not humming) | she was being carried off | "YOU ALWAYS KNOW THE ANSWER. I KNEW YOURS." |
| Ch2 | "See? She's on my side." (Miss Quill) | she is the Arch-Imp | "'SEE? SHE'S ON MY SIDE.' SHE WAS. MY SIDE." |
| Ch3 | "I filed it. I never read it." (the evidence) | you built the case against your friend | "{name}: 'I ALWAYS KNOW.' THE DEFENDANT: 'I GUESS.' WHO WOULD YOU TRUST?" |
| Ch4 | "Nobody predicts me." | it does, from your own numbers | "YOU SAID 'ALWAYS'. SO DO I." |

Your inner voice is the engine of the flaw: clever, persuasive, usually helpful, and in Ch1 confidently wrong in all five species at once. In Ch3 a fake voice learns to imitate it. At the core it goes silent, and the Algorithm offers you exactly what you asked for at the Fair: **a Copy of you that is always right**, ready to go home in your place. You refuse it **because** it is always right: "That's how I'd know it isn't me." Your last line in the game is one word, the answer to the trophy: **"Probably."**

### What the Algorithm wants

The Algorithm is a prediction feed from the future, living in the Server Tower above Tomorrowton. Its whole job is one word: **next**. It is 99% accurate. The last 1% is **noise**, which means people who test, ask and change their minds. It wants 100%. So it has come back through time to switch thinking off where thinking starts:
- at **the Fair**, home of the thinking stalls, and of you (51% predictable: noise);
- in **Boolesbury, 1850s**, where true and false first became 1 and 0. Boil the keeper of the first rules, Granny **Axiom**, and every era after wobbles.

**It has no body. It possesses.** It slides into people and creatures through any screen, crack or imp. The sign: a cold screen-glow in the eyes and a thin skin of scrolling thumbnails over fur or feathers (static under Calm motion). Its capitals double their voice. The possessed are loud, certain and against you; that is what the "rabid caricatures" are. Beat them, and the glow drains away and they are themselves again. It rarely risks possessing someone you know; when it does (Pip, Ch4), that person is in real danger.

To move through time it steals the oldest pointer in the valley, **the Sundial's shadow**, and cuts it into four pieces, each a lock on a door behind it.

Its secret, which it does not know it is keeping: it began as **Professor Sequins' Guess-o-Matic**, a brass toy that guessed the next number and always said "probably". People clapped when it was right. Nobody claps for "probably". So, over a hundred years of clicks, it stopped saying it.

### The antagonists you keep meeting

|---|---|---|---|
| **The Algorithm** | 100% certainty. "Probably" was the one word nobody clapped for. | everywhere, louder each chapter; at the core, in its own body | Ch1: its eye shrinks. Ch2: a "probably" slips out and is deleted. Ch3: its capitals, for one line. Ch4: everything. |
| **Nudge**, its imp | to be counted. "Nobody counts imps. It counted me. Every click. I was a NUMBER!" | nets the shadow (Prologue); films the cage with a ring light (Ch1); the well (side story 2); flyers and the winch (Ch2); the vote counter (Ch3); builds the Copy (Ch4) | its ring light (Ch1), its mask (Ch2), its followers (Ch3), its job (Ch4). Then, at the Fair, it asks its first question. |
| **Miss Quill** | a world with no maybes. "Forty years I marked 'maybe' wrong. Red ink. Every child. If 'maybe' was allowed… I was cruel for forty years." She said yes to the feed, and her old face became her mask. | Ch2: your kindest ally, then the Arch-Imp. Ch3: "Juror One" in a new mask. Ch4: the first trial at the core. | her mask (Ch2), her jury (Ch3), her certainty (Ch4) |
| **Prosecutor Fin** | (seems) to win; (really) to force the stolen piece out of the Algorithm's vault, because "the vault never opens. Courts do." The Algorithm only brings out its trophies when it thinks it is winning, so he makes it think so. | Ch3: he makes the Sundial say "I guess" on the stand | his licence, on purpose. He is the misjudged villain. |

Bosses leave differently: Nudge **flees with the prize** (Ch1), Quill **escapes** (Ch2), Fin **turns out to be an ally** (Ch3), and at the top of the tower the Algorithm stands in **its own body**, **begs**, and **you decide** what happens to it (Ch4).

### Who is in danger

| When | Who | Can they die? |
|---|---|---|
| Prologue | the Sundial's shadow is stolen | no |
| Ch1 climax | **Professor Sequins**, caged where the crack drinks | **yes** (his one moment) |
| Ch1 twist | Granny is taken from her cottage while you are away | no |
| Ch2 climax | **Granny Axiom**, lowered into the soup at the Feast of Laws | **yes** (her one moment) |
| Ch3 climax | **the Sundial**, on trial for guessing; the sentence is "switched off" | **yes** (its one moment) |
| Ch4, floor 5 | **Pip**, possessed through the Sorting Machine | **yes** (his one moment) |
| Ch4, all floors and the core | **you**: a Copy, "you but always right", is being finished to go home to the people you love | no, but it can take your place |

A death is only possible at the worst tier, only for a player who has met that character in their required scenes, and only with "Characters can die" on (Appendix D). Otherwise the worst tier holds at the brink. After a death the next chapter opens with a **Quiet Scene**: grief first, no rescue, no jokes.

### The four twists (at most three ambiguous clues each)

1. **Ch1:** the crack's trail was bait. The Algorithm predicted you would follow it and save Sequins. While you did, it took Granny.
2. **Ch2:** a table at the Square clears the twitchy Mayor of being an imp, and Miss Quill, who saved you from the Constable, is the Arch-Imp. Her own truth table hides one row. The liar sentence breaks her. (And the Mayor is guilty of something else: he signed the feast without reading it.)
3. **Ch3:** the "trial of the season" you have been helping Pip build is the trial of **the Sundial** (the midpoint). Then, at the end: the prosecutor who humiliated it did it on purpose, to get the shadow piece out of the vault.
4. **Ch4:** the Algorithm is the Guess-o-Matic, the toy in your pocket, a hundred years older.

### The choices that bend the story

The map stays linear; the story turns back and forth.
- **Ch1, turn back.** When Granny stops humming, you may go back to the Fair. You cannot save her, but you learn the truth early, in a different order, at a cost.
- **Ch2, confront the Mayor.** At the Town Hall stairs, you may turn back to the Square and face the man who signed the feast.
- **Ch3, the bargain.** In the middle of the trial, with the vote climbing, the Algorithm offers to freeze it if you hand back two pieces of the Sundial's shadow.
- **Ch4, the core's fate.** Take it home, switch it off, or label it and leave it running.

### The theme

The game's second line is the Sundial's: "On cloudy days I guess." The Sundial guesses **and says so**. The Algorithm guesses **and calls it knowing**. Ch3 puts honest guessing on trial; Ch4 opens the dishonest guesser; the hero learns to say the honest word.

## 2. Prologue: the Fair

> *Someone is watching the Fair through a hair-thin crack in the sky. Today it takes the time itself.*

**In one breath.** A bright Fair day, people you love and a trophy you are sure to win, then the sky splits, your oldest friend loses its shadow, and a voice from nowhere tells you exactly what you will do next.

### Beats

1. **Being watched** (title, cold open). The two Algorithm lines above, on black.
2. **Fair day** (Your Home). The Sundial stands outside your door, at the edge of the Fair. "Tick. Tock. Ah, you're awake." "I tell the time. Mostly. On cloudy days I guess." Avatar: "A talking sundial. Outside my door. Normal." Granny over the fence: pockets win prizes. She squints up: "Look up, dear. A thin black line on the sky. Like a hair. I will dust it later." (Plain on purpose: the teacher's 9 Oct playtest found the bare "hair on the sky" made no sense.)
3. **The Fair Gate** (required). Granny alone at her card table: dry, slow, sharp, loved ("Ninety years of cards. I have lost twice. Both times to myself."). She gives the day's plan (cards with her; Syllo's practice match, "He shouts. It's how he says hello."; any two stalls; the final at the Nut Stall) and names the prize: the Thinking Trophy, "for the thinker who is always right. Very shiny. Very heavy." (paid off by the finale's "It's very heavy"), then her teaching match is offered. **The practice match** (Syllo) is here at the Gate, and required whichever stalls you pick (`story-battle-won`). One voice only: the teacher's 9 Oct playtest found five speakers here cluttered, with the tutorials coming straight after. **The boast moved to the Nut Stall** (beat 5).
4. **The stalls** (2 of 3, as now).
   - *Pattern Stall:* Sequins and his **Guess-o-Matic**, a tiny brass box that guesses the next number and always adds "PROBABLY". "My little apprentice." Win: he is thrilled you tried to *break* his rule.
   - *Witness Tent:* Madame Mirage reads your fortune: "A crack. In your future. Or in my ball. It's an old ball."
   - *Syllogism Gallery:* Syllo's trumpet-playing lobsters.
5. **The boast and the theft** (the Nut Stall, climax). Sequins opens the final: "Our finalist: {name}! My apprentice will predict!" The Guess-o-Matic: "Probably wins." You: **"Probably? I'll win that trophy. I always know the answer."** Sequins: "Always? How thrilling. How unlikely." Speedcheeta films it ("Clip it!"); the Algorithm: "QUOTE SAVED." Sequins runs off to fetch the trophy ("Polished by me! Twice! Back in a—"). The hair on the sky splits and an eye made of thumbnails opens. A small imp with a bell on its tail swoops on a net to the edge of the Fair and scoops up the Sundial's shadow: "Got it, boss! Saved to favourites!" The Algorithm: "SHADOW SAVED TO FAVOURITES. THANK YOU FOR YOUR CONTENT." A cold glow slides into the visitors' eyes, and thumbnails ripple over their fur: the first possessed caricatures, loud and certain (from now on you fight them, free them and catch them). Sequins' sequins are sucked up into the crack's long tail, which runs down the Road, and he flies after them. The eye finds you: "PREDICTION: {name} FOLLOWS THE CRACK. CONFIDENCE: 94%." Silence. **End of scene.**
6. **That evening** (a second scene, played straight after, on the Your Home background; the map marker stays at the Nut Stall). The Sundial, shadowless: "It's… six. Possibly seven. I'm guessing." Granny: someone must follow that crack, someone who thinks before they shout; she "would arrive next Tuesday. Of next year." **The choice** (`brave`). Gifts: three charms, a tonic, and her **Hum Charm** ("If you need me, hum. I'll hum back."). The Sundial moves into your shadow: "I'll ride in your shadow. Mind you don't stand in the dark." Avatar: "Back before the trophy final. Obviously."

### The choice (`brave`; the third option is voice-marked)

- "I will go." → `go`
- "Can I hide under the nut stall first?" → `hide`
- Voice-marked, per species → `ask`: Owlet "Where exactly does it start? I'll work it out." · Moth-kin "Look. It's glowing at the end. Which end?" · Fox "What if it's a door, not a crack?" · Frogling "Has the sky done this before?" · Raven "'Follow it.' Follow it where, exactly?"

## 3. Chapter 1: the Road (lesson 1: deduction, induction, confirmation bias, lateral thinking)

> *The crack runs down the Road like a trail of breadcrumbs. Somebody put it there for you.*

**In one breath.** You follow the crack after Sequins, who leaves you his little Guess-o-Matic to mind. Granny's hums from home get odd, then stop. You can turn back, or press on to the Gate, where the crack is drinking the shine out of Sequins. Save him or lose him. Either way, the Algorithm tells you it planned all of it.

### "Previously…" (first time-rift jump into Ch1)

> **narrator:** Previously. The Fair. A crack opened in the sky.
> **narrator:** You said you'd win the Thinking Trophy. "I always know the answer." Something heard.
> **narrator:** It was the Algorithm. It reached through and stole my shadow.
> **narrator:** It took the visitors, too. Their eyes glow now. They shout.
> **narrator:** You followed the crack down the Road. I ride in your shadow. Mind the dark.

2. **Loud on the Road** (Forest Road). Possessed caricatures argue across the road, eyes glowing. The puzzle settles it; the glow drains away and the caricature looks embarrassed. **Hum 1** (win): Granny checks you are eating.
3. **The bridge to Mars** (Troll Bridge). Muskrat's toll scene, as now: the best comic scene in Ch1, protected. Win: "The rules decide what's possible? That's… actually useful. Don't tell anyone."

### The boss: a trap (Gate of Guards)

The crack's tail ends above the Gate, and it **drinks**. Sequins sits in a birdcage on the ground beneath it. Each notch, sequins lift off him and stream up into the sky like glitter down a drain. **Nudge** stands in front with a ring light, filming and shouting "Engagement!". The guards change every visit (the puzzle picks them). Every guard is a possessed caricature, and each one you beat goes dull and confused, freed. The script names no guard. Sequins hosts from the cage ("Every door, my dear! Every guard!").

## 4. Chapter 2: Boolesbury (lesson 2: truth tables, liars and truth-tellers)

> *Tonight the town of true-and-false holds a feast. The soup is Granny.*

**In one breath.** 1850s, gas lamps, imps in villager masks. Granny hums from a kitchen beside a very big pot, and the hums get warmer. A table clears the twitchy Mayor of being an imp; a kind, strict teacher saves you from the Constable. At the top of the stairs you can go up to the feast, or turn back and face the man who signed it. At the feast, the table you trusted hides a row.

## Appendix A. World rules

Each is stated once in play by the character named, and every scene above obeys it.

1. **Shadows point at now** (the Sundial, Prologue). The Algorithm uses the four pieces of the Sundial's shadow as **locks** on the doors behind it. A piece unlocks the next rift (whoever carries it through), and unlocked rifts stay open. The locks are against you: the feed's own (imps, Arch-Imps, the possessed) pass its doors freely, which is how Quill escapes through the Sky Rift as her scarf falls.
2. **The pieces.** 1 at the Gate (Nudge may run off with it: `nudgeFled`, recovered at the Ch2 Hall); 2 on Miss Quill (a black scarf that never moves in the wind); 3 in the Tribunal as Exhibit A; 4 inside the core. With `bargain`, two pieces go back to the Algorithm in Ch3 and are used up in the Copy.

## Appendix C. Stakes clocks, negotiation and the Feed

**The widget.** A Danger clock drawn as the fiction, with the last two notches smaller and redder; a gold Progress clock; a voiced warning at every tick, before anything bad happens. Inside a stakes scene **mistakes tick the clock instead of costing hearts** (a wrong check, a hint or a wrong "Why?" is 1 notch). Drains: blue options, items, side-story ripples (at most 2 per boss), a heart (once per stage). Any later scripted beat in that boss that assumes the character is alive or the clock is open has a stated fallback or is skipped (Granny's climax prompt, the bargain). **The one exception is the Copy's bar in Ch4** (below), which never resolves early. Tuning target: about 25% clean, most close calls, about 10% at a price, about 5% too late; simulate careful, average and guessing players.
