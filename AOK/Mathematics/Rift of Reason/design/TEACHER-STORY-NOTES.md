# Teacher's story direction (binding for STORY.md, SIDE-STORIES.md, UNDERSTUDIES.md)

Collected from the teacher's messages. Writers follow these; critics check them.

## 8 October 2026

1. **Stakes.** Put established, loved characters in real danger (e.g. Granny lowered into a pot), with gradient outcomes, not win/lose.
2. **Deaths and understudies.** Every NPC has an understudy, or their station goes dark if they are minor. Understudies stay off stage unless the original's role is vacated (death, disablement, another plot reason). They are never used elsewhere.
3. **Sequins may die at the first boss.** The writer's call; keep it.
4. **After a death: solemn, not a rescue.** No heroic "rescue" framing. The next chapter has a quiet scene that always happens: gathering their things, their last words, the people who miss them, pain, and then moving on. Real grief, never mocked by the story's own voice. (The villain may be callous; the game is not.)
5. **Recurring antagonists.** Bosses are not one-scene villains. Meet them, and their minions, throughout as irritants: they cause trouble, mock you, get in the way, and we learn why they do it. When beaten they run away or lose something; a final showdown pays off that investment. Not the same pattern every lesson.
6. **Not everyone cast as a villain is one.** Logic puzzles can show someone in a bad light that turns out to be false. Some "villains" do something with a short-term cost for a long-term good (an ethical question) and have been vilified for it.
7. **Choice and non-linear storytelling.** Don't railroad. The player may decide the boss is not the real problem: e.g. turn back to town to confront the Mayor, who is the one driving everyone towards disaster. The game stays linear in structure, but the story can go back and forth, with dramatic reveals.
8. **Side stories** are short one-shots at revisited stations (blinking icon) that make students reason: negotiation, deduction, spotting bad arguments. They are not main plot. Example: a criminal trapped at a fair station holding hostages.
9. **The Algorithm possesses.** It is not just a voice: a parasitic, bodiless force that takes over people and creatures and turns them against you, with a clear visual cue (a glow or skin that shows something is wrong). Possessing an NPC we know is a way to put them in danger: confronting them can kill them and bring in their understudy. Rules: only NPCs whose understudy is not yet active can be possessed; an NPC still has at most one death-risk moment in a playthrough, so if a possession uses it, every other beat that could kill them is locked out (and vice versa). Don't overuse the trick: possessed creatures and caricatures can carry most of it, freed from its hold when beaten. In the final confrontation you face the Algorithm in its own form.
10. **Quality gate.** Three critics (logic, author, editor) must each score 8/10 or more (`WRITING-CRITICS.md`).
