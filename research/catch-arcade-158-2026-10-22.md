# Two real-time catch games for #158: wall-building and rock-splitting

Research date: 2026-10-22. Addendum to [catching-arena-158-2026-10-18.md](catching-arena-158-2026-10-18.md), following the teacher's 18 October decisions on [#158](https://github.com/RktRobinhood/TOK-Materials/issues/158): rebuild Pocket Nets as a JezzBall-style game, and add an Asteroids-style fifth game. [Verified] means checked against the linked source. [Builder] means our own design call or measurement. No classroom measurement is claimed.

## Wall-building: JezzBall and KBounce

**[Verified]** KBounce, KDE's open-source take on JezzBall, says "the goal of the game is to fill at least 75% of the field and advance to the next level" ([KDE handbook, introduction](https://docs.kde.org/stable5/en/kbounce/kbounce/introduction.html); [apps.kde.org listing](https://apps.kde.org/el/kbounce/)). Its [rules file](https://lxr.kde.org/source/games/kbounce/RULES) adds: a life is lost "when a ball hits the inner of a wall"; "a wall is finished when it is hit by a ball at its end or it hits a black block"; and players "can always build two 'half' walls concurrently". KBounce is GPL, so we used it as a design reference only and copied no code, text or art.

What makes the genre work, and what we kept:

- **One rule, many layouts.** You grow a wall and close off empty ground. The fill meter shows progress, so you can see why you won or lost.
- **Risk you can read.** A wall is only in danger while it grows. A player can judge "no creature is heading here" from the screen.
- **Difficulty from a few numbers:** ball count, speed and the fill goal. No new rules per level.

**[Builder] Our version (Pocket Nets).** A 12×9 meadow with rocks, trees and ponds. Each moving portrait is the visiting creature; more portraits at higher rarity (2/3/4/5). Drag or tap to grow a net; it grows both ways until it meets terrain, closed ground or the edge. A creature touching a growing half breaks it: one net lost per touch. Closed ground with no creature is netted. **A creature boxed into six squares or fewer is penned, and the pen counts as closed.** This is the fix for the teacher's 2×3 report: a small enclosure is now progress, never a dead end. Terrain is solid, so it shortens nets and makes good cuts easier. One net grows at a time, not KBounce's two halves, which keeps touch play simple. Goal: 80% of the field within 60 s, with 3 nets.

## Rock-splitting: Asteroids

**[Verified]** In Atari's *Asteroids* (1979), designer Ed Logg's concept was a shooter where rocks break into smaller pieces when hit ([Wikipedia overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroids_(video_game))). Large rocks split into two smaller rocks with different trajectories, and the smallest pieces do not split ([TV Tropes summary](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Asteroids); secondary sources, since no original manual scan was found). Screen edges wrap.

What makes it work: every hit makes the field busier before it gets emptier, so priority matters (break the closest threat), and the arena reads at a glance.

**[Builder] Our version (Shard Burst).** The player's lantern sits in the centre; there is no ship to steer, because steering needs a second control on a phone. Tap, click or hold to throw a net at the pointer; arrow keys aim and Space throws. A big rift shard splits into two medium, and each medium into two small. Every shard shows the visiting creature. Break every shard to catch it. Shards wrap at the edges (like Asteroids) and bounce off the lantern, which costs one of four shields with a one-second grace. Only one net is in the air at a time. Measured: with three in flight, holding fire toward the shards won about 98%, and even random spraying won 45–75% at common. With one net, random spraying wins 0–8%, so aim decides the round.

## Calm and reduced motion

**[Builder]** Both games share one deterministic 60-tick clock. In Calm motion (or the system's reduced-motion setting) the field stands still and shows each mover's next second as a dotted ghost path. It moves for half a second only after your move or a **Wait** press. That is the "turn-based version" the teacher asked for on 17 October. It is easier than real time, and it is reported separately.

## How the 50% target was checked

**[Builder]** Each served layout is admitted only if a skilled test player wins it without help. That win is replayed as a certificate. The win rate was tuned against a documented **average sensible player** instead, with human reaction times, a few spots weighed per decision, misjudged speed, missed cells and aim error. Results and settings are in `AOK/Mathematics/Rift of Reason/design/reviews/catch-arcade-158-2026-10-22.md`. These are machine numbers, not classroom success rates.
