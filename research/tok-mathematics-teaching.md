## 1. Summary: top 10 ideas

These ideas fit the IB guide best, fill the biggest gaps in the current materials and work well as games. All are detailed in the catalogue (section 4).

1. **"Patterns that betray you": Moser's circle (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, then 31).** This extends your existing 2-4-8 slide. Students draw it by hand and almost all predict 32. The point is that induction gives no certainty in maths, which is why maths needs proof. It also prepares the Collatz/Goldbach "checked billions of times, still unproven" beat. [Wikipedia: Moser's circle problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moser%27s_circle_problem); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/how-pizza-can-solve-this-unusual-math-puzzle/)
2. **Lakatos' *Proofs and Refutations* as a role-played dialogue.** Students "prove" Euler's V - E + F = 2 and then the class finds counterexamples (a picture frame, a cube with a cube-shaped hole). This is the best single activity for showing that maths knowledge is produced by a community and gets revised. It suits a "Proof Forge" chapter or the Tribunal. [Wikipedia: Proofs and Refutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proofs_and_Refutations)
3. **The parallel postulate and non-Euclidean geometry (Saccheri to Bolyai/Lobachevsky to Einstein).** It shows what an axiomatic system is, that "obvious" axioms can be swapped, and that "useless" pure maths later became physics. It could be a time-travel era. Your Intro slide 13 already says "when axioms break".
4. **Who judges a proof? Wiles' 1993 gap, Mochizuki's abc "proof", the four colour theorem, and 2025–26 AI/Lean proofs of Erdős problems.** This covers four guide KQs directly (the community's role, technology, certainty, "who judges the validity of a proof?"). It works as a boss or tribunal case. [Quanta: abc clash](https://www.quantamagazine.org/titans-of-mathematics-clash-over-epic-proof-of-abc-conjecture-20180920/); [Quanta: Erdős problems falling to AI (Aug 2026)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/)
5. **Liar paradox to Gödel.** One villager in Truth-table village says "I am lying", and no row of the truth table works for it. This leads to Gödel's "this statement is unprovable" and to the KQ on truth versus certainty. It is the natural boss of Truth-table village. [SEP: Liar paradox](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/liar-paradox/); [SEP: Gödel's incompleteness theorems](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/)
6. **Monty Hall, played live with cups, followed by the vos Savant letters story.** Intuition loses to reasoning, experts (including Erdős) were wrong, and a simulation (empirical evidence) convinces people when the proof did not. It serves the guide KQ on conclusions that conflict with intuition. [Wikipedia: Monty Hall problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monty_Hall_problem)
7. **Data dredging trial.** Tyler Vigen's spurious correlations, Simpson's paradox (Berkeley 1973), and the Sally Clark case (the prosecutor's fallacy). The guide names "data dredging" in its ethics KQs, and statistics in the media is its core-theme link for maths. This fits the Argument tribunal. [Spurious correlations](https://www.tylervigen.com/spurious-correlations); [RSS statement on Sally Clark (2001)](https://rss.org.uk/RSS/media/File-library/Membership/Sections/2020/Sally-Clark-RSS-statement-2001.pdf)
8. **Danish algorithm ethics: Udbetaling Danmark's welfare-fraud algorithms (Amnesty, *Coded Injustice*, 2024), plus the UK 2020 A-level algorithm.** A local, current case for the guide's KQs on mathematicians' responsibility. [Amnesty International](https://www.amnesty.org/en/documents/eur18/8709/2024/en/)
9. **Hilbert's Hotel and Cantor's infinities, with Zeno's Achilles and the Tortoise.** Rigorous maths gives counter-intuitive results. The Tortoise can be a recurring NPC (Zeno, then Lewis Carroll's "What the Tortoise said to Achilles" on the Logic road). [SEP: Zeno's paradoxes](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paradox-zeno/); [Wikipedia: What the Tortoise Said to Achilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_the_Tortoise_Said_to_Achilles)
10. **Ethnomathematics and perspectives: Ishango bone, Maya and Indian zero, Babylonian base 60, Tshokwe *sona* sand drawings (Euler paths!).** This serves the guide KQ on whether maths is embedded in cultures, and it maps easily onto time-travel eras. *Sona* can be a hands-on puzzle that is literally graph theory. [Demaine et al., Sand drawings and Gaussian graphs](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/Sona_BRIDGES2006/paper.pdf); [D'Ambrosio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubiratan_D%27Ambrosio)

**Bonus Danish hooks:** Tycho Brahe's data from Hven, Kepler's 8-arcminute discrepancy and the ellipse (why maths is so effective in describing the world). Piet Hein's game **Hex** (1942): Nash proved that the first player can always win but gave no winning strategy, which is a non-constructive proof (a formalism vs intuitionism hook).

**Structural suggestion:** **Königsberg** can serve as a recurring "hub city" in the time-travel story. It hosts Euler's bridges (1736, birth of graph theory), Kant (maths as synthetic a priori knowledge), and the September 1930 conference where Gödel announced incompleteness one day before Hilbert's "We must know, we will know" radio address.

## 3. What the IB guide asks for (TOK guide, first assessment 2022)

Source: IBO *Theory of knowledge guide* (2020), "Areas of knowledge: Mathematics", pp. 18–22 of the AOK extract (`IB/TOK/Resources/AOKs - Knowlwdge Framework.pdf`). The KQs below are abbreviated paraphrases. The guide says they are suggestions, not prescriptive.

**Framing (guide narrative).** Maths is often seen as uniquely certain and as built on more or less universally accepted definitions and assumptions. The guide suggests four discussion foci:
1. **Status of maths:** why the human sciences cast conclusions in mathematical form, why maths signals "rigour", and why schools privilege maths.
2. **Creativity, imagination, beauty and elegance:** why elegance should matter to mathematical value.
3. **Maths and the world:** maths models reality yet is abstract, based on reason rather than observation.
4. **Proof and truth:** whether "proof" means something different in maths, in everyday life and in other AOKs.

**Example KQs by knowledge-framework element (abbreviated):**

- **Scope:** why maths is so important to other AOKs (especially the natural sciences); how computing has changed the scope of maths; whether absolute certainty is attainable; truth vs certainty; whether maths should be defined as a language; whether maths is defined by its subject matter or its method; whether maths yields knowledge of the world only when combined with other AOKs; whether there is a hierarchy of AOKs by usefulness.
- **Perspectives:** why results stay unchanged over time; how much notable individuals have mattered; the role of the mathematical community in validating proofs; whether maths is embedded in cultures or traditions; the role of personal experience; whether progress is harder in maths; whether maths made by humans can still give objective facts about the world.
- **Methods and tools:** mathematical vs scientific reasoning; what "proof" means; how mathematicians handle conclusions that conflict with intuition; what an axiomatic system is and how it compares with other knowledge systems; whether symbols mean things the way words do.
- **Ethics:** whether high status brings special responsibilities; criteria for holding mathematicians responsible for unethical applications; how "data dredging" is used to mislead; whether pure research without applications is justifiable; whether mathematical and ethical judgements face similar evidential problems; who should write codes of ethics for mathematicians.
- **Core-theme links (Knowledge and the knower):** why maths has privileged status in education; who judges the validity of a proof; how to avoid being misled by statistics in the media; Paul Ernest's claim that maths trains thinking in an "ethics-free and amoral way".

**General guide expectations that matter for unit design:**
- All four framework elements must be covered for every AOK.
- Comparisons across AOKs are a key focus and should be *evaluative*, not descriptive. For example: proof in maths vs evidence in the natural sciences vs sources in history.
- The focus must stay on **knowledge** (KQs), not on teaching maths or logic content for its own sake.
- Assessment relevance: the essay needs specific, AOK-located real-life examples, and maths often appears in prescribed titles. Exhibition objects can come from maths too (e.g. a Lean proof screenshot, a betting slip, a welfare-algorithm letter).

**Teacher-community consensus** ([theoryofknowledge.net](https://theoryofknowledge.net/free-tok-notes/areas-of-knowledge/mathematics/), [tokresource.org](https://www.tokresource.org/4-mathematics), [toktopics.com](https://toktopics.com/maths-materials-handouts-activities/), [TOK@EIS](https://sites.google.com/view/tokeis/areas-of-knowledge/mathematics)). The units these sources describe usually include:
- invented vs discovered (Platonism/formalism)
- proof vs intuition (Monty Hall)
- paradoxes (liar, barber, infinities, non-Euclidean geometry)
- induction and conjecture (Collatz, Pascal)
- Ramanujan as the intuitive knower
- maths as a language and cultural maths (Mayan numerals, Pirahã "can't count past four")
- human number perception and probability bias
- statistics and risk (Bayes/mammograms, the hot hand, Rosling's *Factfulness* quiz)
- algorithms and ethics (Cathy O'Neil, predictive policing, the UK A-level algorithm)
- lottery ethics

The 2026–27 theoryofknowledge.net unit is framed around invented/discovered, elegant patterns misleading us, and neutral-looking metrics hiding bias ([classic maths lessons page](https://theoryofknowledge.net/tok-resources/classic-tok-lessons/classic-mathematics-lessons/)).

## 5. Gaps between current approach and guide/sources

1. **Proof is underdeveloped.** The guide treats proof and its relation to truth as a central focus. Current lessons cover deductive validity and truth tables, but students never build, break or judge a mathematical *proof*. Add Moser, Lakatos, a proof by contradiction (√2) and the Wiles/abc/AI cases.
2. **Logic is not maths.** The planned chapters lean heavily on formal logic (syllogisms, truth tables, fallacies). Much of that belongs to Knowledge and the Knower or methods. The guide's maths KQs barely mention logic puzzles. Each logic chapter needs an explicit bridge KQ to maths: "what is an axiomatic system?", "do symbols mean what words mean?", "truth vs certainty" (liar → Gödel). Otherwise the unit risks becoming a logic course.
3. **"Who judges a proof?" (perspectives / community) is missing.** This is a guide core-theme KQ. Wiles, Mochizuki, the four colour theorem and AI/Lean fill it.
4. **Technology and scope (computing, AI) is missing.** The guide asks directly how computing has changed maths. The 2025–26 AI proofs of Erdős problems are an unusually current example ([Quanta, Aug 2026](https://www.quantamagazine.org/why-the-legendary-erdos-problems-are-falling-to-ai-20260803/); [Quanta on Tao, Jun 2026](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-terry-tao-became-an-evangelist-for-ai-in-math-20260608/)). Your Intro slide 5 ("Does any of this relate to AI?") is a seed to grow.
5. **Intuition vs counter-intuitive results.** This is a guide M&T KQ. Monty Hall, infinity, Banach–Tarski and Saccheri appear in the glossary but have no activity yet.
6. **Cultural perspectives exist only in the glossary.** The ethnomathematics and Maya/Babylon entries need an activity (*sona*, number systems, Pirahã).
7. **Ethics coverage is broad but misses the guide's specific angles.** It does not yet address data dredging (named in the guide), responsibility for applications (Hardy/RSA, welfare algorithms), whether pure research is justifiable, or codes of ethics (Chiodo). The existing dating/Pareto/polls material is good for engagement, but it should be pinned to these KQs.
8. **Notable individuals** (a guide perspectives KQ) are largely absent. The time-travel format is ideal for this: Euclid, Hypatia, al-Khwarizmi, Brahmagupta, Tycho/Kepler, Euler, Gauss, Noether, Ramanujan, Gödel, Turing, Mirzakhani.
9. **Cross-AOK comparison** should be evaluative, as the guide emphasises. Build it explicitly into the finale (maths proof vs scientific evidence).
10. **Minor content fix:** the "180° triangle = axiom" example (see section 2).
